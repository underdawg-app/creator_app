## 1. Discovery mechanisms: how platforms expose new creators, with evidence of effect

### Takeaway
Every major platform now has *some* mechanism that guarantees new work a first audience independent of follower count (TikTok's interest graph, Instagram's small-audience-first ranking and Trial Reels, SoundCloud First Fans, YouTube Hype's small-channel bonus, Spotify Fresh Finds), but almost none publish independent evidence of effect, several are paywalled or drifting toward pay-for-reach (SoundCloud tiers, Spotify Discovery Mode, YouTube paid-hype tests), and the curated/human lanes (Behance, DeviantArt, Fresh Finds) are small relative to upload volume. Instagram and ArtStation have both publicly admitted their own ranking historically favoured large accounts.

### Inferences
- **[INF]** "Guaranteed first audience + staged expansion" is now an industry-standard pattern (TikTok, Instagram 2024, Trial Reels, First Fans). Underdawg cannot differentiate by merely having one; it can differentiate on *who* the first audience is (curators, bookers, brands and peers in the right discipline/city) and on making it free and transparent.
- **[INF]** The industry trend is to monetise the "fair shot" (SoundCloud subscription gating, Discovery Mode commissions, YouTube paid-hype tests). A credible "no pay-for-reach in discovery surfaces" rule is a positioning opportunity — but it constrains the Pro-plan revenue design.
- **[INF]** In India the strongest small-creator discovery engines are Instagram (481M reach) and YouTube (500M; Hype since July 2025). Underdawg is competing with incumbents that already market "small creator boosts" in India.
- **[INF]** Human curation lanes work best with explicit anti-concentration rules (DeviantArt's one-feature-per-year cap) and fail when curation keys off "traction" (Behance) or follower-weighted votes (ArtStation).

## 2. Popularity bias and fair-discovery techniques (input to brief section 6, "Discovery impact")

### Takeaway
Academic and industry evidence agrees that recommender systems amplify already-popular items (a "rich get richer"/Matthew effect), and two platforms (Instagram, ArtStation) have admitted it publicly. Mitigation techniques exist — exploration batches, newcomer bonuses, exposure caps, editorial rotations, provider-fair re-ranking — but the research base is almost entirely offline simulation (one field test in a 123-paper survey). The most useful field-adjacent evidence is Spotify's own: fairness-aware *adaptive* ranking did not have to cost satisfaction, and more diverse listening correlates with much better retention and conversion. Underdawg would need to run its own experiments.

### Cited Findings
**Academic evidence**
- Survey of 123 papers (Klimashevskaia, Jannach, Elahi, Trattner; *User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction*, 2024): recommender systems promise long-tail visibility but "in many situations today's recommendation algorithms instead exhibit a popularity bias"; reinforcement is described as "rich get richer", "sometimes referred to as 'Matthew Effect'"; mitigation falls into pre-processing, in-processing (e.g., popularity terms in the loss function) and post-processing (re-ranking is "the most common post-processing technique", including an xQuAD adaptation by Abdollahpouri et al. 2019) **[V]** — [arXiv 2308.01118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01118); [DOI 10.1007/s11257-024-09406-0](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/s11257-024-09406-0).
- Same survey: evaluation is "very strongly dominated by offline experiments"; only four works report a user study (all 2015 or earlier) and "a single work was found which examined popularity bias effects in a field test (Lacic et al. 2022)"; the literature relies on "certain assumptions regarding the practical effects of including long-tail items" **[V]** — [arXiv 2308.01118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01118).
- Spotify Research (Mehrotra, McInerney, Bouchard, Lalmas, Diaz; Oct 2018, CIKM): artist exposure is "superstar"-skewed; "very few recommended sets have both high relevance & high fairness"; adaptive policies that personalise based on each user's affinity for fairer (long-tail) recommendations gave "gains of 9-21% in satisfaction estimates" while keeping fairness **[V; counterfactual offline estimates]** — [Spotify Research](https://research.atspotify.com/towards-a-fair-marketplace-trade-off-between-relevance-fairness-satisfaction-in-recsys/).
- Anderson, Maystre, Anderson, Mehrotra, Lalmas (Spotify/U. Toronto, WWW 2020): user-driven ("organic") listening "is typically much more diverse than algorithm-driven listening"; users who become more generalist do so "by drifting away from algorithmically-driven listening"; controlling for activity, "generalist users are up to 25 percentage points less likely to leave the platform and up to 35 percentage points more likely to become a paid subscriber"; relevance matters "much more" to specialists than to generalists **[V]** — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~ashton/pubs/alg-effects-spotify-www2020.pdf). (A widely repeated "12% diversity reduction" figure seen in a search summary does **not** appear in this paper — do not use it.)

**Platform admissions and concentration data**
- Instagram (2024): "creators with large followings and aggregators of reposted content have gotten more reach in recommendations than smaller, original content creators" **[V]** — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html).
- ArtStation (2017): trending became "a popularity contest"; "really hard for newcomers... because they have fewer followers" **[V]** — [ArtStation Magazine](https://magazine.artstation.com/2017/05/updated-trending-algorithm-diversity-quality/).
- YouTube, Jan 2019 (older data): among 43,770 channels with 250k+ subscribers, "Just 10% of the popular channels... produced 70% of all the videos" and the top 10% of videos by views "were responsible for 79% of all the views" **[V]** — [Pew Research Center, 25 Jul 2019](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2019/07/25/a-week-in-the-life-of-popular-youtube-channels/).
- Spotify (21 Nov 2023): "99.5% of all streams are of tracks that have at least 1,000 annual streams"; the tens of millions of tracks below that averaged "$0.03 per month"; from early 2024 tracks need ≥1,000 streams in 12 months to earn recorded royalties **[V]** — [Spotify for Artists](https://artists.spotify.com/en/blog/modernizing-our-royalty-system).

### Inferences — discovery impact principles for Underdawg (brief section 6)
- **[INF] P1. Guaranteed, free first exposure for every new work** to a small, discipline- and taste-matched audience that *includes demand-side users* (curators, bookers, brand scouts) and peers; expand in stages on quality signals (saves, shares, curator picks), never on follower count. Directly targets the zero-plays problem.
- **[INF] P2. Newcomer bonus with a hard ceiling**: an inverse-audience boost (Hype-style) that switches off above a size threshold, so large accounts can't use the platform's fairness surfaces.
- **[INF] P3. Exposure caps and rotation on every curated or trending surface** (e.g., one featured slot per artist per period, à la DeviantArt), published openly.
- **[INF] P4. Do not weight votes, likes or endorsements by the voter's follower count** (the ArtStation lesson); if weighting is used, base it on verified domain expertise (e.g., curators, working professionals), and cap any single endorser's influence.
- **[INF] P5. No paid placement in organic discovery surfaces.** Clearly label any paid promotion and keep it in separate slots (avoids the Discovery Mode payola backlash). This affects Pro-plan design: Pro can sell tools, not reach.
- **[INF] P6. Measure exposure fairness as a product KPI**: share of impressions and of opportunity-giver views going to artists below a follower threshold, Gini of exposure, % of new uploads reaching ≥N relevant viewers in 72h — alongside diversity of what users consume (the Anderson et al. retention link).
- **[INF] P7. Personalise the fairness dose** (Mehrotra 2018): some users and opportunity-givers tolerate more long-tail exposure than others; adaptive mixing beats a fixed quota.
- **[INF] P8. Prefer intent/skill/locality relevance over popularity** for demand-side search (brands and bookers searching "Hindi indie vocalist, Pune, available March" should not get follower-sorted results).
- **[INF] P9. Run online A/B tests**: the literature has almost no field evidence, so Underdawg must measure whether fair exposure produces *outcomes* (bookings, curator adds, commissions) and not only views.
- **[INF] P10. Every feature should be checked for "does this reward already-popular artists?"**: tips leaderboards, fan clubs, follower-sorted search and "top artist" badges all compound the Matthew effect unless capped or separated from discovery ranking.

### Gaps
- No reliable public data found on the share of Instagram or TikTok views going to the top 1% of creators (the Pew data is 2019 YouTube and covers only channels with 250k+ subscribers).
- No published A/B results from any platform's newcomer boost (First Fans, Hype, Trial Reels) with a control group.
- Exact Spotify Discovery Mode commission rate: not on the official page; FMC's 50% claim is unconfirmed; a commonly cited ~30% figure was not verified this session.

## 3. Curators and gatekeepers (curator marketplaces, A&R tools, editorial)

### Takeaway
Paid curator marketplaces are large and growing (Groover 600k+ artists; SubmitHub 1.6M users and 48.5M submissions; Musosoup 85k artists), which is strong evidence that artists will pay for *guaranteed attention* from gatekeepers. The dominant model makes artists pay per submission (Groover €2 per curator; Musosoup £42 per campaign), with roughly a 31% approval rate at SubmitHub. Bandcamp Clubs show a reverse model where fans pay the curator and the artist. A&R scouting increasingly runs on data tools that track artists *already* on major platforms (Chartmetric 14M+ artists, Soundcharts 20M), so pre-traction artists are invisible to data-led scouting. No India-specific curator marketplace data was found.

### Inferences
- **[INF]** Willingness to pay for curator attention is proven (hundreds of thousands of artists; millions of submissions). The pain is "nobody with influence listens to my work", not a lack of upload outlets.
- **[INF]** Illustrative arithmetic from verified prices: pitching 20 Groover curators at 2 Grooviz each costs €40; a SubmitHub approval rate of 31% means about 69% of submissions are declined. These marketplaces sell *consideration and feedback*, not exposure, and the artist bears the cost.
- **[INF]** Groover's figures (1M+ shares versus 4M+ feedbacks) imply roughly one share per four feedbacks. Both are lower bounds, so the true ratio could be higher or lower. It is a derived ratio, not a reported acceptance rate.
- **[INF]** An opportunity for Underdawg is a curator review queue where curators are recruited and rewarded by the platform, or by fans and brands as in Bandcamp Clubs, with guaranteed response SLAs, rotation and caps. Making artists pay per submission would import the payola critique. Whether Indian curators (playlisters, blogs, festival programmers, gallery scouts) would join is untested.
- **[INF]** Data-led A&R tools only see artists who already have streaming and social traction. A pre-traction signal layer (curator picks, peer endorsements from working professionals, opportunity outcomes) is something those tools cannot see. It is a plausible B2B "talent scouting" product, but only after the network has density.

### Gaps
- No verified, independent critiques of Groover, SubmitHub or Musosoup (e.g., artist complaints about low-effort feedback or "pay-to-pitch payola") were gathered: the search budget was exhausted and Reddit was blocked. These need checking before claiming a "common complaint".
- No data on Indian curator marketplaces, Indian playlist curators, or Indian A&R scouting practices. No reliable public data found this session.
- Casting/agency platforms (Backstage, Mandy, Indian casting platforms) were blocked or not reachable. No verified data.

## 5. Local scenes and offline discovery

### Takeaway
Offline discovery works through curated live formats (Sofar: 22,000+ shows in 350+ cities by 2019) and through festival and college-fest pipelines in India (Mood Indigo's Livewire drew 200+ bands in 2012 and launched Parikrama and Motherjane). Tooling for live discovery (Bandsintown, Spotify concert surfacing) mostly pushes shows to people who already follow or stream the artist, so it doesn't help unknown artists find bookers or audiences. Sofar's model drew labour scrutiny, which is a warning for any "showcase" feature that relies on volunteer or under-paid labour.

### Cited Findings
- **Sofar Sounds**: intimate shows, usually three artists, with secret venues revealed close to showtime; company-run and ambassador-run cities "organizing roughly one or two shows per month"; raised $25M in May 2019 (Battery Ventures, Union Square Ventures) on top of $6M earlier; "over 22,000 performances" in "more than 350 cities" by Nov 2019 **[2nd]** — [Wikipedia: Sofar Sounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofar_Sounds). Investigated in 2019 by the New York Department of Labor over unpaid labour; the settlement required it to "stop using volunteer workers"; it then introduced paid "Sofar Crew" and "flat guarantees and higher payment tiers based on ticket sales" for artists at company-run shows **[2nd]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofar_Sounds); headline confirmed: "Sofar Sounds Will Augment Show Volunteers With Paid Crew, Amid Department of Labor Investigation" (16 Sep 2019) **[V headline only]** — [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/articles/business/8529961/sofar-sounds-department-of-labor-investigation-augment-volunteers-paid-crew).
- **Bandsintown for Artists**: "Loved by 700,000+ artists & teams worldwide"; pitch line "Fans aren't finding out about your shows"; promise "Reach fans across platforms, sell out shows faster" **[V/CC]** — [artists.bandsintown.com](https://artists.bandsintown.com/).
- **Spotify live**: Spotify has driven more than $1.5B in gross concert ticket sales; in 2025 "nearly 25% more artists used Spotify to reach fans with concert offers"; it works by "surfacing nearby shows to real fans already streaming an artist's music" **[CC]** — [Loud & Clear](https://loudandclear.byspotify.com/takeaways/).
- **India: festivals.** NH7 Weekender (launched Pune 2010 by OML, now organised by NODWIN Gaming) mixes "Indian independent acts, younger sub-continental artists, and multi-genre international headliners"; cumulative attendance exceeded 110,000 across three cities in 2016 **[2nd]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NH7_Weekender). ### Inferences
- **[INF]** Local discovery tools serve the fan-to-show direction (Bandsintown, Spotify) but not the booker-to-unknown-artist direction. "Find an emerging act in my city, genre and budget for a date" is underserved, especially in India, where pipelines are competitions, fests and personal networks.
- **[INF]** A city/scene layer (city pages, upcoming local shows by emerging artists, open-mic and competition calendars, booker search by city) would give Underdawg a local-discovery advantage that global platforms do poorly. It needs supply partnerships with venues, fests and promoters.
- **[INF]** Any Underdawg showcase or event product must pay artists and avoid volunteer-labour models (the Sofar precedent).

## 7. Authenticity and AI verification

### Takeaway
Demand for "human-made" signals is strong and growing on both sides. Artists downloaded Glaze 3.5M–6M+ times and Nightshade 0.7M–1.6M+ times (2024 figures) and moved en masse to Cara. Listeners want labels: in a 9,000-person Deezer survey, 80% want fully AI music labelled and 97% couldn't tell AI from human music. Supply-side pressure is extreme: AI made up more than half of Deezer's daily uploads at peak in mid-2026. Platforms are reacting with bans (Bandcamp, Jan 2026), detection (Cara/Hive, Deezer tagging) and provenance standards (C2PA, Adobe Content Credentials). Verification is technically fragile, though: detection is an arms race, Cara discontinued featured Glaze support (September 2026) and was scraped (August 2026).

### Cited Findings
- **Cara**: uses "third-party content moderation services from Hive to prevent AI-generated art from being uploaded"; won't "host AI-generated portfolios unless the rampant ethical and data privacy issues around datasets are resolved via regulation"; believes AI content should be "clearly labeled" so "the public should always be able to search for human-made art" **[V/2nd]** — [Cara About](https://cara.app/about); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara_(app)). Growth 40k → 650k users in a week after Meta's notice that public posts would train its AI from 26 Jun 2024; founder Jingna Zhang said her name appeared "20,000+ times in MidJourney" **[V]** — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/06/a-social-app-for-creatives-cara-grew-from-40k-to-650k-users-in-a-week-because-artists-are-fed-up-with-metas-ai-policies/). Glaze "featured support... discontinued as of September 2026" **[V]** — [Cara blog](https://blog.cara.app/blog/cara-glaze-about/). On 13 Aug 2026 a Reddit user claimed to have scraped a 12-terabyte archive (12 million works) from Cara **[2nd]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cara_(app)) citing [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/08/14/tech-bro-scrapes-anti-ai-photo-app-cara-then-gloats-about-it/).
- **Glaze/Nightshade adoption**: Sept 2024: Glaze downloaded "nearly 3.5 million times", Nightshade "over 700,000" **[V]** — [MIT Technology Review, 10 Sep 2024](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/09/10/1102936/innovator-year-shawn-shan-2024/). Nov 2024: Glaze "downloaded more than 6 million times since it launched in March 2023", Nightshade "more than 1.6 million" **[V]** — [MIT Technology Review, 13 Nov 2024](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/11/13/1106837/ai-data-posioning-nightshade-glaze-art-university-of-chicago-exploitation/). (The two figures, two months apart, are hard to reconcile. Treat them as a range.) The Glaze team's survey of "more than 1,200 artists" found 97% expected AI to decrease job security for some creators, about a quarter said AI art had already affected their jobs, and "more than half said they anticipated reducing or removing" their online work **[V, as reported]** — [MIT TR, Nov 2024](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/11/13/1106837/ai-data-posioning-nightshade-glaze-art-university-of-chicago-exploitation/).
- **Bandcamp AI ban (13 Jan 2026)**: prohibits "Music and audio that is generated wholly or in substantial part by AI" and "use of AI tools to impersonate other artists or styles"; enforcement relies on community reporting; aims to ensure "fans have confidence that the music they find on Bandcamp was created by humans" **[V]** — [Bandcamp blog](https://blog.bandcamp.com/2026/01/13/keeping-bandcamp-human/).
- **Deezer (July 2026)**: ~90,000 fully AI-generated tracks uploaded per day; over 50% of daily uploads at peak; AI music is 1–3% of streams; up to 85% of AI-music streams were fraudulent in 2025. Listener survey (Nov 2025, 9,000 respondents, 8 countries): "97% couldn't tell the difference", "80% agree that 100% AI-generated music should be clearly labeled", "73%... would like to know if a music streaming service is recommending 100% AI-generated music", "52%... feel that 100% AI-generated songs should not be included in charts" **[V/CC]** — [Deezer newsroom, Jul 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/).
- **Not By AI badges**: "if you estimate that at least 90% of your content is created by humans, you are eligible"; paid plans "Starting at $5/mo. Or... a one-time $99 fee" (free for non-commercial use); its adoption counter displayed "0K+ pages", apparently a rendering error, so there is no usable adoption number **[V]** — [notbyai.fyi](https://notbyai.fyi/).
- **C2PA / Content Credentials**: C2PA provides "an open technical standard for publishers, creators and consumers to establish the origin and edits of digital content", described as "like a nutrition label for digital content"; steering-committee logos include Google, Meta, OpenAI and TikTok **[V]** — [c2pa.org](https://c2pa.org/). Adobe Content Authenticity (beta) lets creators attach verified name and social accounts to files (up to 50 at a time), "Ask AI not to use your content" via a training preference, and recover credentials through "invisible watermarks and digital fingerprinting" **[V]** — [contentauthenticity.adobe.com](https://contentauthenticity.adobe.com/).

### Inferences
- **[INF]** "Human-made, verified" is a strong trust layer for curators, brands and buyers on an artists-only platform. It is also a discovery filter, because opportunity-givers can search for verified human work. Supporting evidence: listener labelling demand (Deezer), artist protective behaviour (Glaze downloads), and migration events (Cara).
- **[INF]** Detection alone is fragile (97% of listeners can't tell; detectors trail generators). A more defensible approach combines process evidence (WIP/timelapse/stems/layer files), C2PA credentials at upload, peer and curator attestation, and community reporting (the Bandcamp model). Claims should be scoped as "evidence of human process", not "guaranteed no AI".
- **[INF]** Anti-scraping promises are risky. Cara's protections were circumvented in 2026. Underdawg should avoid over-promising AI-training protection and instead offer opt-out signalling (e.g., Content Credentials' training preference) plus clear terms.

### Gaps
- Demand from visual-art *buyers* or brands for human-made verification (surveys): no reliable public data found this session.
- Adoption counts for C2PA credentials among independent artists, and display support on Instagram/LinkedIn as of 2026: not verified.
- Detection accuracy of Hive or other detectors on artwork: not verified.

## 8. Positioning map and least-occupied territory (brief section 23), plus differentiation inputs (brief section 22)

### Takeaway
Competitors cluster in three crowded territories: creative community (Behance, Dribbble, DeviantArt, VSCO, SoundCloud, Patreon), monetisation/identity link hubs (Linktree 70M+, Beacons 10M+, Stan, Patreon), and hiring for polished professionals (Behance, Dribbble, Contra). Discovery claims are listener- or viewer-facing ("Discover amazing music"), not about getting artists seen by opportunity-givers. Fair exposure for emerging artists exists mostly as features of incumbents (Hype, First Fans, Fresh Finds, Trial Reels) rather than as anyone's core positioning; VSCO ("exposure for all photographers") and Audiomack ("youth-driven, artist-first") come closest. ### Inferences
- **[INF] Least-occupied territory:** "Fair discovery for under-discovered artists, routed to the people who create opportunities (curators, bookers, brands, collaborators), across disciplines, India-first." It combines the emerging-talent, opportunity and collaboration territories, which the checked competitors treat separately. Defensibility depends on (1) a demand-side network that does not exist yet, (2) trust rules (no pay-for-reach, human-made verification) that incumbents with ad and royalty models find hard to adopt, and (3) proprietary pre-traction signals (curator picks, peer endorsements, opportunity outcomes) that data-led A&R tools can't see.
- **[INF] Why it may be empty:** marketplaces for unknown talent face a cold-start problem on the demand side. The Cara precedent ("all creatives sharing with each other") shows that an artists-only community without buyers, clients or curators struggles to deliver exposure. Incumbents can also copy fairness features cheaply (Hype, Trial Reels), so features alone are not a moat.
- **[INF] Differentiation inputs (brief section 22), answered against the evidence:**
  - *Why join if already on Instagram?* Under the current plan (tools layered on Instagram), there is **no strong answer**: the tools complement Instagram rather than replace anything it does, and link-in-bio is commoditised (Linktree 70M+, Beacons 10M+). A strong answer would be "Instagram admits it historically favoured big accounts and has no opportunity infrastructure; Underdawg guarantees your work reaches relevant curators, bookers and brands, without paying for reach."
  - *Instead of Behance?* Behance curates once per project, rewards "traction" and polish, and leans toward design/hiring. A possible answer is cross-discipline coverage (music, visual, performance), India-local opportunities and rotation rules that favour newcomers. It is **weak until the demand side exists**.
  - *Instead of TikTok?* TikTok is banned in India (since 29 June 2020), so India-first Underdawg does not compete with it domestically. Globally, Underdawg cannot out-algorithm TikTok's cold-start engine. The answer has to be context (opportunities and curators), not virality.
  - *Instead of Cara?* Both are artist-first. Cara's weak point is the lack of a non-artist audience, and its AI protections were bypassed in 2026. Underdawg's possible answer is the demand side plus India focus plus multi-discipline coverage plus opportunity routing. If Underdawg stays artists-only with no demand side, it inherits Cara's weakness.
  - *Musicians already on SoundCloud/Spotify?* Those platforms own listening. Their fair-shot features are paywalled (SoundCloud) or editorial-capacity-bound (Fresh Finds Forward ≈10k/yr), and their reach tools are pay-to-play (Discovery Mode). Underdawg's possible answer covers non-streaming outcomes (gigs, collaborations, brand and sync briefs, local shows) and curator access without pay-per-submission.
  - *Why would creative professionals keep the app installed?* Only if it reliably delivers paid opportunities, relevant new contacts or guaranteed feedback from gatekeepers. Groover (600k+ artists) and SubmitHub (1.6M users) show artists pay for that. A feed of artists alone would not justify it.
  - *"You need to be on Underdawg"* would plausibly be said only after outcomes: "I got booked, commissioned or featured through it" or "every piece I post is seen by real curators in my field." **The current plan does not create these sentences.**

- The Dots' and ArtStation's current official taglines could not be retrieved.
- No India-specific competitor positioning (e.g., Indian creator or artist platforms) was gathered this session. Other researchers' competitor notes should fill this.
- No consumer or artist survey tests which territory resonates (positioning is inferred from claims, not demand).

## 9. Missing features (brief section 14): mechanisms Underdawg has not considered, each tied to an evidenced artist problem

### Takeaway
The current plan has no mechanism that exposes unknown artists to new audiences or opportunity-givers. The highest-value missing features are (1) a fair-exposure first-audience queue with newcomer bonus and caps, (2) a demand-side layer (curator review queue, booker/brand skill-and-location search, scouting dashboard), (3) an India-first, fee-transparent opportunity board, and (4) a human-made trust layer. Peer critique, challenges, credits, local scenes and showcases follow behind them and are valuable only if they feed discovery.

- **[INF] Priority logic.** Features 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 form the minimum discovery system: supply exposure, fairness rules, gatekeeper access, opportunity supply and demand-side search. - **[INF] Combination opportunity.** The curator queue (#3), the critique circle (#10) and challenges (#11) can share one "review" primitive, since in each case someone credible looks at work and can nominate it into the fair-exposure queue (#1). ## 10. Does the current Underdawg plan deliver discovery?

### Takeaway
**No, not as described.** The current direction (link-in-bio, DM automation, Art Shop, Live Rooms, Fan Club, tips, downloads, bookings, tickets, Hire Artists, Gigs, Pro plan, layered on Instagram, with no in-app discovery feed) is a monetisation and conversion toolkit for artists who already have an audience. It leaves discovery entirely to Instagram's algorithm, which Instagram itself says historically favoured large accounts. Only Hire Artists, Gigs and Bookings could create discovery, and only if there is a demand side and a fair, non-follower-based ranking. The plan currently helps the already-discovered more than the under-discovered, which inverts the core objective.

### Cited Findings
- **DM automation cannot reach new people.** Meta's Instagram messaging API: "Only after an Instagram user has sent your app user's Instagram professional account a message can your app send a message to the Instagram user", within a standard messaging window that "For most user actions... lasts 24 hours"; messages from non-followers land in "Requests" **[V]** — [Meta for Developers: Instagram Messaging API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-instagram-login/messaging-api/).
- **Link-in-bio is commoditised and positioned on monetisation, not discovery**: Linktree "Join 70M+ creators and sell, share & curate everything you do online" [linktr.ee](https://linktr.ee/); Beacons "Join 10M+ creators... grow your brand and monetize your work with a custom link in bio" [beacons.ai](https://beacons.ai/); Stan "We work 1-on-1 with the Top Creators to monetize their business" [stan.store](https://stan.store/) **[V/CC]**.
- **Instagram's discovery is controlled by Instagram and historically skewed**: "creators with large followings and aggregators... have gotten more reach in recommendations than smaller, original content creators" **[V]** — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html). In India, Instagram reaches 481M and YouTube 500M (late 2025) **[V]** — [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-india); TikTok is banned **[2nd]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_of_TikTok); YouTube Hype has targeted small Indian creators since July 2025 **[V]** — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/youtube-rolls-out-hype-feature-in-india-to-boost-smaller-creators-145430.html).
- **Artist-only communities without a demand side struggle to deliver exposure**: "the problem with an app like this seems to be that it's all creatives sharing with each other" **[V]** — [Creative Boom on Cara](https://www.creativeboom.com/resources/everyones-talking-about-cara-but-is-it-any-good/).
- **Selling the fair shot draws a backlash**: SoundCloud gates First Fans/promotion by subscription tier **[V]** — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/soundcloud-launches-3-25-a-month-artist-tier-targeting-emerging-and-aspiring-musicians/); Spotify Discovery Mode faced payola criticism and litigation **[V]** — [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2026/05/05/spotify-discovery-mode-payola-lawsuit-to-move-into-arbitration/).

**Feature-by-feature discovery check ([INF] analysis based on the facts above)**

| Planned feature | Creates exposure to people who don't already know the artist? | Who it actually reaches | Discovery verdict [INF] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Link-in-bio | No | Visitors already on the artist's Instagram profile | Monetisation/conversion only; commoditised |
| DM automation | No (API allows replies only after the user messages first) | Already-engaged followers/commenters | Engagement/conversion only |
| Art Shop / downloads / tips / Fan Club / tickets | No, unless listed in a browsable cross-artist marketplace | Existing fans | Monetisation; tips and fan leaderboards can amplify the Matthew effect |
| Live Rooms | Only if surfaced to non-followers via a discovery surface | Existing followers by default | Potential discovery, but only with a fair-exposure surface |
| Bookings / Hire Artists / Gigs | Yes, if brands, bookers and agencies are on the platform and ranking is by fit, not followers | Opportunity-givers | The only real discovery lever in the plan; needs demand-side acquisition and fair ranking |
| Pro plan | Neutral; becomes harmful if it sells reach | Paying artists | Must sell tools, not visibility (P5) |

### Inferences
- **[INF] Core contradiction.** Monetisation tools deliver value roughly in proportion to an artist's existing audience, so the already-popular gain most. Without a discovery layer, Underdawg would be a "better Linktree for artists", which competes with Linktree (70M+), Beacons (10M+) and Stan, does not serve the under-discovered, and does not match the brand promise.
- **[INF] Minimum changes to deliver discovery** (smallest set): (1) an in-app fair-exposure surface (first-audience queue + newcomer bonus + caps, see principles P1–P5); (2) verified demand-side accounts (curators, bookers, brands) with skill/location search and a curator review queue; (3) an India-first opportunity board linked to profiles; (4) a human-made trust layer. The existing monetisation tools become downstream conversion for artists after they are discovered.
- **[INF] India-specific risk.** Incumbents (Instagram, YouTube with Hype) already market small-creator boosts in India. Underdawg should not try to beat them on reach. It should differentiate on routing artists to opportunities and gatekeepers, which incumbents do not do.

### Gaps
- No usage data for comparable "tools layered on Instagram" products showing whether they generated new audiences for small creators. No reliable public data found.
- Instagram API rate limits and policy changes affecting automation (2025–2026) were not checked beyond the messaging-window rule.

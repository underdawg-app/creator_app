# Indian evidence on the problems of Indian creators and artists: income, discovery and reach, payments, scams and rates

Research date: 2026-10-02. India only. Every number below was read on the page or PDF linked beside it unless it is marked "not opened" or "unverified". Company-published numbers are labelled "company claim". Amounts are in rupees; USD is given only where the source used USD. 1 crore = 10 million; 1 lakh = 100,000.

Reading note on source quality:
- Primary documents opened in full: EY "State of Influencer Marketing in India" (PDF, 2024), EY-IPRS "The music creator economy" (PDF, 2023), IPRS Annual Report FY2025-26 (PDF), FICCI-EY M&E report March 2026 (PDF), DPIIT Working Paper on Generative AI and Copyright (PDF, December 2025), PIB release on the BCG report (May 2025), DataReportal Digital 2022 to 2026 India pages, YouTube blog (Neal Mohan, WAVES 2025), Meta newsroom (Edits Film Festival).
- Kofluence, Qoruz, HashFame, KlugKlug, StarClinch and OpraahFx are vendors in this market. Their figures are estimates or surveys of their own users and are labelled as company claims.
- The BCG report PDF and several press sites (Business Standard, Deccan Herald, Mint, Forbes, exchange4media, NewsBytes) blocked access. Where a number is known only through a secondary report, that is stated.

## 1. Income: what do Indian creators and artists earn?

### Takeaway
Most Indian creators earn little or nothing: BCG (via PIB) says only 8 to 10% of India's 2 to 2.5 million creators "monetize effectively", Kofluence estimates 450,000 to 600,000 monetising creators out of 3.5 to 4.5 million, and 88% of creators get less than three-quarters of their income from social media. For musicians there is hard data: 69% of authors and composers paid by IPRS received under ₹25,000 a year, and only 60% of surveyed music creators can do music full-time. No Indian income survey exists for visual artists, illustrators, dancers or photographers.

### Cited Findings

**Share who monetise, share who earn little**
- India has 2 to 2.5 million "active digital creators, defined as individuals with over 1,000 followers"; "only 8–10% of them currently monetize their content effectively". Creator-ecosystem direct revenues are estimated at USD 20–25 billion today, projected to USD 100–125 billion by 2030; creators influence USD 350–400 billion of consumer spending (BCG, "From Content to Commerce: Mapping India's Creator Economy", launched 3 May 2025 at WAVES) — [PIB press release, 2 May 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2126106); same figures in [DD News, May 2025](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/waves-2025-indias-creator-economy-set-to-influence-over-1-trillion-in-consumer-spending-by-2030-says-bcg-report/)
- Wording conflict: BCG's own landing page calls the 2 to 2.5 million "monetized content creators", while PIB and DD News say 2 to 2.5 million is the active base and only 8 to 10% monetise effectively — [BCG publication page, 3 May 2025](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/india-from-content-to-commerce-mapping-indias-creator-economy); contradicted by [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2126106)
- Kofluence estimates 3.5 to 4.5 million influencers in India, of whom "an estimated 450K to 600K creators are currently monetising their content in some form"; by platform: Instagram 1.8 to 2.3 million, YouTube 500K to 700K, other platforms 90K to 120K (company claim; Sreeram Reddy Vanga, CEO, Kofluence) — [IBTimes India interview, 8 July 2025](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/how-much-do-creators-really-earn-kofluence-ceo-decodes-indias-influencer-economy-interview-885077)
- 46.3% of creators identify as full-time content creators; 88% earn less than 75% of their total income from social media (Kofluence "Decoding Influence 2026", company survey) — [Storyboard18, 14 May 2026](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/46-creators-are-full-time-influencers-but-most-still-rely-on-side-income-streams-kofluence-report-98018.htm)
- Sponsored collaborations are the primary income for about 50% of creators; platform revenue (AdSense, Super Thanks) for about 15% (Kofluence, company claim) — [IBTimes India, 8 July 2025](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/how-much-do-creators-really-earn-kofluence-ceo-decodes-indias-influencer-economy-interview-885077)
- 84.4% of creators say short-form video (Reels, Shorts) is their highest-monetising content format (Kofluence 2026, company survey) — [Storyboard18, 14 May 2026](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/93-brands-prioritise-instagram-as-84-creators-monetise-best-through-short-form-video-kofluence-report-98028.htm)
- "India has over 8 million creators" but "nearly 200,000 have exited since 2024"; "only 5–10% of creators earn over ₹50,000 per month"; Indian creators earn USD 0.40 to USD 3 per 1,000 YouTube views; "most of the earnings go to the top 1%" (Outlook Business web story citing Mint; the Mint original could not be opened; page undated) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/ampstories/news/india-loses-2-lakh-creators-amid-burnout-low-payheres-what-you-need-to-know)
- The Nod Mag reports that 8 to 10% of influencers can monetise, that the majority of monetised creators earn under ₹18,000 a month, and that "India lost 2 lakh creators amid burnout and low pay" (secondary; attributes to BCG and press, the ₹18,000 figure was not found in the PIB release) — [The Nod Mag, Rhea Arora, 7 August 2026](https://thenodmag.com/content/indian-content-creator-influencer-economy-struggles)
- EY survey (556 creators, September to October 2023): 77% of influencers reported income growth in the past two years; 86% expected over 10% income growth in the next two; "12% of influencers earn between INR1 and 10 lakhs" (the PDF does not state whether per month or per year); 73% work less than 10 hours a week on content — [EY, "State of influencer marketing in India", April 2024, p.14](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/ey-state-of-influencer-marketing-in-india-03-04-2024.pdf)

**Income by tier (Kofluence, company claims)**
- FY 2024-25 short-form video revenue: nano (1K–10K) ₹300 to ₹5,000; micro (10K–100K) ₹3,000 to ₹1,00,000 on Instagram and up to ₹2,00,000 on YouTube; macro (100K–500K) ₹75,000 to ₹5,00,000; over 500K "often earn more than" ₹3,00,000. The interview does not say whether these are per post, per month or per year, and calls them "estimates, and widely varying" — [IBTimes India, 8 July 2025](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/how-much-do-creators-really-earn-kofluence-ceo-decodes-indias-influencer-economy-interview-885077)
- "Indian creator earnings range from ₹20,000 to ₹200,000" monthly on average; Instagram mega influencers ₹3 to 5 lakh per collaboration (company claim) — [Kofluence blog, dated 3 May 2024](https://www.kofluence.com/blog/how-much-money-indian-creators-really-make/)
- Share of YouTube creators who monetise, by tier: nano (1K–10K subscribers) 10% (20,000–30,000 people); micro (10K–100K) 30% (30,000–60,000); macro (100K–500K) 85% (60,000–68,000); mega (over 500K) 95% (6,000–10,000); total 110,000 to 170,000 Indian creators monetising on YouTube (company claim) — [Kofluence blog, dated 3 May 2024](https://www.kofluence.com/blog/how-much-money-indian-creators-really-make/)
- Masoom Minawala Mehta (1.3 million followers) is reported to charge ₹3,00,000 to ₹5,00,000 per post — [The Nod Mag, 7 August 2026](https://thenodmag.com/content/indian-content-creator-influencer-economy-struggles)

**YouTube payouts and earnings**
- YouTube "paid over INR 21,000 crore to Indian creators, artists, and media companies in the last three years" and committed "over INR 850 crore in the next two years" — [YouTube Official Blog, Neal Mohan at WAVES, 5 May 2025](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/neal-mohan-creator-economy-waves-2025/)
- YouTube India Impact Report 2025 (Oxford Economics): YouTube's creative ecosystem contributed ₹18,000 crore to India's GDP in 2025 and supported 960,000+ full-time-equivalent jobs; channels earning ₹10 lakh or more a year grew 30%+ year on year; 73% of creators cite YouTube as primary income source (base of that percentage not stated in the coverage) — [Marketing Mind, 30 September 2026](https://marketingmind.in/youtubes-creator-economy-contributes-over-rupees-eighteen-thousand-cr-to-indias-gdp/); The420 reports the same report as "more than 20%" growth in channels earning ₹10 lakh+ — [The420.in, 30 September 2026](https://the420.in/youtube-india-creator-economy-gdp-jobs-2025/)

**Musicians (best Indian artist-income data found)**
- IPRS-EY survey of 500 music creators (2022): 87% want to live off music alone but only 60% make music full-time as a career; 19% make money from music alongside a job; 8% used to; 12% never have — [EY, "The music creator economy: The rise of music publishing in India", December 2023, survey chapter](https://iprs.org/wp-content/uploads/ey-the-music-creator-economy.pdf); summary in [EY press release, 5 December 2023](https://www.ey.com/en_in/newsroom/2023/12/ey-survey-reveals-that-just-60-percent-of-music-creators-can-pursue-their-careers-on-a-full-time-basis)
- Annual payout per author registered with IPRS (percentage of recipients): 69% received under ₹25,000 a year; 7% ₹25,000–50,000; 17% ₹50,000–6 lakh; about 6% in total received more than ₹6 lakh (three bands: ₹6–12 lakh, ₹12–24 lakh, over ₹24 lakh) — [EY, "The music creator economy", December 2023, p.27 chart](https://iprs.org/wp-content/uploads/ey-the-music-creator-economy.pdf)
- Top revenue source (number of the 500 respondents ranking it first): live performances including DJing 139; upfront fees from labels and film producers 129; publishing royalties 53; background scores and session work 51; self-released music 39; brand sponsorships 8; others 25 — [EY, "The music creator economy", December 2023, survey chapter](https://iprs.org/wp-content/uploads/ey-the-music-creator-economy.pdf)
- "Most authors depend on one-time, on-call opportunities such as session work, live performances and creating content for music labels or film producers, as the primary sources of income"; "Most Indian authors currently have almost no social security net" — [EY, "The music creator economy", p.27](https://iprs.org/wp-content/uploads/ey-the-music-creator-economy.pdf)
- Only 56% had access to the hardware and software needed to produce quality music; 35% reinvest more than 50% of music earnings in equipment (30% reinvest 25–50%, 18% reinvest 10–25%, 17% under 10%); 73% strongly feel they have much to learn about production and 56% about monetisation — [EY, "The music creator economy", December 2023, survey chapter](https://iprs.org/wp-content/uploads/ey-the-music-creator-economy.pdf)
- EY estimates India has about 40,000 music creators producing over 20,000 original songs a year; music publishing revenue was ₹884 crore in FY 2022-23 — [EY press release, 5 December 2023](https://www.ey.com/en_in/newsroom/2023/12/ey-survey-reveals-that-just-60-percent-of-music-creators-can-pursue-their-careers-on-a-full-time-basis)
- IPRS FY 2025-26: collections ₹804.5 crore (₹741.6 crore in FY 2024-25; ₹169.8 crore six years earlier); royalties distributed ₹628.3 crore to "more than 13,700 members"; membership 23,943; royalties paid by year: FY21 ₹184.6 crore, FY22 ₹210.1 crore, FY23 ₹304.2 crore, FY24 ₹501.5 crore, FY25 ₹608.8 crore, FY26 ₹628.3 crore — [IPRS Annual Report 2025-26, September 2026](https://iprs.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual_Report_FY2025-26.pdf)
- FICCI-EY, March 2026: Indian music segment revenue ₹59 billion (₹5,900 crore) in 2025 (₹46 bn in 2022, ₹54 bn in 2023, ₹53 bn in 2024); YouTube revenue to music fell 8% in 2025 "as the platform prioritized YouTube Shorts"; "reduced per-stream rates and higher minimum stream count thresholds reduced revenues for some creators"; paid music subscribers about 14.4 million; public-performance licensing has "a 4% compliance rate pan-India" and generates "a paltry INR5 billion" (industry executive quoted in the report; name not legible in the extract) — [FICCI-EY Media and Entertainment Report, March 2026](https://aidcf.com/wp-content/uploads/FICCI-EY-Media-and-Entertainment-Report-2026_reduced.pdf)
- Gig fees quoted by an Indian booking platform (company claim): beginner (0–1 year) ₹3,000–10,000 per gig; intermediate (1–3 years) ₹10,000–40,000; professional ₹50,000–2,00,000+; cafés from ₹3,000, weddings ₹1,00,000+ — [StarClinch blog, 16 December 2025](https://starclinch.com/blog/how-to-find-live-music-gigs-in-india-complete-guide-for-emerging-artists/)
- Indie artists (Gutslit's Gurdip Singh Narang, Tejas, Pratika Prabhune, Adil Manuel) say club gigs pay a "token fee", that fees are "not enough to cover costs spent on gear", and that all keep side income (production, voiceovers, ads, teaching); 2022 data — [Rolling Stone India, David Britto, 13 July 2022](https://rollingstoneindia.com/heres-what-its-really-like-to-be-an-indian-indie-artist-playing-live-gigs/)
- Opening acts in India "are rarely paid fairly"; the column proposes a floor such as ₹10,000 for a club slot (opinion) — [Rolling Stone India, Peony Hirwani, 12 September 2025](https://rollingstoneindia.com/what-the-music-industry-doesnt-talk-about-enough-minimum-wage-for-musicians)
- Per-stream payouts (compiled estimates by an Indian distributor, not primary data): ₹0.08–0.12 per stream on free-tier Indian platforms, ₹0.18–0.25 on premium Indian platforms; 10,000 to 50,000 streams a month needed for ₹1,000–5,000 monthly income — [The Black Turn Research, 6 June 2026](https://theblackturn.com/research/state-of-indian-independent-music-2026/)

**Dancers**
- Bollywood background dancers earned ₹4,000–4,500 for a day of song shooting (₹3,900–4,000 in hand after deductions) and ₹750 per rehearsal shift; Cine Dance Association had about 800 members; films per month had fallen from 20–30 to 10–15 (2020 data) — [The Tribune, 20 May 2020](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/as-music-fades-bollywoods-background-dancers-look-for-help-to-survive-87450/)

**Illustrators and visual artists**
- India has no illustration agencies of the kind that negotiate fees and chase invoices, no India-specific rate guide, and commissioned work can default to client ownership under Section 17(b) of the Copyright Act 1957 (essay by a designer with 15 years' experience; qualitative) — [Aparajitha Vaasudev, "India Has No Illustration Agents", Substack, 19 August 2026](https://studioapara.substack.com/p/india-has-no-illustration-agents)

### Inferences
- Using Kofluence's own ranges, 450,000–600,000 monetising creators out of 3.5–4.5 million is roughly 10 to 17% (about 13% at the midpoints). That is consistent with BCG's 8 to 10% "monetize effectively", so "roughly one in ten Indian creators earns from it" is a defensible India-first line.
- IPRS paid ₹628.3 crore to about 13,700 members in FY26, a mean of about ₹4.6 lakh per paid member, but the mean includes large publishers and is skewed; the EY chart (69% under ₹25,000 a year) is the better picture of the typical writer or composer. Only about 57% of the 23,943 members received any royalty in FY26.
- YouTube's ₹21,000 crore over three years averages about ₹7,000 crore a year, but it is shared among "creators, artists, and media companies", so it overstates what individual small creators receive.
- For Indian musicians, live gigs and one-off fees (not streaming or royalties) are the main income, which makes gig discovery and getting paid for gigs the core problem.

### Gaps
- No reliable Indian data found on incomes of visual artists, illustrators, photographers or dancers (no Indian equivalent of the UK artist-livelihood surveys). The only dancer pay figure is from 2020; Glassdoor-type salary pages were not used.
- No Indian source gives the share of creators earning exactly zero. The closest are "8–10% monetise effectively" (BCG) and tier-wise YouTube monetisation shares (Kofluence).
- The widely repeated claim that most monetised creators earn under ₹18,000 a month could not be verified in a primary BCG document (PDF blocked).
- The Mint article behind "2 lakh creators quit" and "8 million creators" could not be opened; treat as secondary.
- Qoruz, Wobb, Zefmo, iCubesWire and ISB reports: no income data from these was found in accessible sources.

## 2. How many creators and artists India has, by tier and by platform; growth by year

### Takeaway
Estimates range from under 1 million to 8 million depending on definition, but every source agrees the base is overwhelmingly small creators: Kofluence puts 61.1% in the nano tier and 32.5% in micro, and Goat/Kantar counts 40.6 lakh influencers in 2024 against 9.6 lakh in 2020.

### Cited Findings
- Influencer count grew from 9.6 lakh in 2020 to 40.6 lakh in 2024, a 322% rise; about 30,000 are mega or celebrity influencers (India Influencer Marketing Report 2025, The Goat Agency (WPP Media) and Kantar) — [MediaBrief, 24 June 2025](https://mediabrief.com/india-influencer-report-2025-goat-kantar-growth/)
- Kofluence 2026: 4.0 to 4.4 million active creator professionals; 3.3 to 3.7 million use Instagram as primary platform; 61.1% are nano (1,000–10,000 followers); 15.2% are registered as formal business entities or GST individuals; methodology: 2 million+ creators analysed, 1,000+ surveys, 50+ interviews (company claim) — [MediaNews4U, 15 May 2026](https://www.medianews4u.com/15-of-indias-creators-are-now-registered-business-entities-as-influencer-marketing-crosses-a-structural-threshold-kofluence/)
- Nano 61.1% and micro (10,000–100,000) 32.5% of the creator market (Kofluence 2026, company claim) — [Storyboard18, 14 May 2026](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/46-creators-are-full-time-influencers-but-most-still-rely-on-side-income-streams-kofluence-report-98018.htm)
- Kofluence 2025: 3.5 to 4.5 million creators in total; Instagram 1.8 to 2.3 million; YouTube 500K to 700K; others 90K to 120K (company claim) — [IBTimes India, 8 July 2025](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/how-much-do-creators-really-earn-kofluence-ceo-decodes-indias-influencer-economy-interview-885077); [Kofluence blog, 27 June 2025](https://www.kofluence.com/blog/2025-influencer-marketing-report-sneak-peek/)
- BCG: 2 to 2.5 million active creators with over 1,000 followers — [PIB, 2 May 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2126106)
- EY (citing Brand Equity and BW, 2023): mega (1 million+ followers) 7K+ influencers; macro (100,000–1 million) 92K+; micro (10,000–100,000) 331K+; nano (100–10,000) 500K+ — [EY, "State of influencer marketing in India", April 2024, p.9](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/ey-state-of-influencer-marketing-in-india-03-04-2024.pdf)
- HashFame uses 4 million creators as its base (company claim) — [MediaBrief, 27 May 2025](https://mediabrief.com/indian-influencers-lost-over-%E2%82%B9350-cr-in-brand-deals-hashfame/)
- "India has over 8 million creators" (Outlook Business citing Mint; original not opened) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/ampstories/news/india-loses-2-lakh-creators-amid-burnout-low-payheres-what-you-need-to-know)
- YouTube: "over 100 million channels in India uploaded content in the past year", "more than 15,000 of these surpassing one million subscribers" — [YouTube Official Blog, 5 May 2025](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/neal-mohan-creator-economy-waves-2025/)
- YouTube channels with over 1 million subscribers in 2026: "18,000+" per [Marketing Mind, 30 September 2026](https://marketingmind.in/youtubes-creator-economy-contributes-over-rupees-eighteen-thousand-cr-to-indias-gdp/); contradicted by "more than 20,000 ... as of June 2026" in [The420.in, 30 September 2026](https://the420.in/youtube-india-creator-economy-gdp-jobs-2025/)
- Music: about 40,000 music creators (EY estimate, 2023); IPRS membership grew from 6,745 six years ago to 23,943 in FY 2025-26 — [EY press release, 5 December 2023](https://www.ey.com/en_in/newsroom/2023/12/ey-survey-reveals-that-just-60-percent-of-music-creators-can-pursue-their-careers-on-a-full-time-basis); [IPRS Annual Report 2025-26](https://iprs.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual_Report_FY2025-26.pdf)
- Age: 44% of YouTube creators and 48% of Instagram creators are aged 18 to 25 (Kofluence 2025, company claim) — [Kofluence blog, 27 June 2025](https://www.kofluence.com/blog/2025-influencer-marketing-report-sneak-peek/)

### Inferences
- If 61.1% are nano and 32.5% micro, about 94% of Indian creators have under 100,000 followers. That is Underdawg's addressable "under-discovered" base: roughly 3.7 to 4.1 million people on Kofluence's 2026 count.
- Kofluence's Instagram creator count jumped from 1.8–2.3 million (2025 report) to 3.3–3.7 million "primary platform" (2026 report) while the total barely moved (3.5–4.5 million to 4.0–4.4 million); the definitions clearly changed, so do not chart these as a time series.

### Gaps
- No reliable Indian data found on the number of visual artists, illustrators, dancers or photographers in India.
- No single source gives a consistent year-by-year creator count; only the Goat/Kantar 2020 and 2024 points are comparable.
- Qoruz and ISB creator-count reports were not found in accessible sources.

## 3. Reach and discovery in India

### Takeaway
Instagram's Indian audience has roughly doubled in three years to 481 million and YouTube is at 500 million, so supply of attention is not the problem; matching is. Half of creators say limited brand-collaboration opportunities are their main obstacle, 83% of marketers say they struggle to discover influencers, and 62% of surveyed creators did not know brands had tried to reach them.

### Cited Findings

**Platform user numbers (DataReportal advertising-reach figures)**
- Instagram India: 230.3 million (early 2022); 229.6 million (early 2023); 362.9 million (January 2024); 414 million (January 2025); 481 million (late 2025, 32.8% of population, +22.9% year on year) — [Digital 2022 India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2022-india); [Digital 2023 India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2023-india); [Digital 2024 India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2024-india); [Digital 2025 India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-india); [Digital 2026 India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-india)
- YouTube India: 467.0 million (early 2022); 467.0 million (early 2023); 462.0 million (early 2024); 491 million (early 2025); 500 million (late 2025, 34.1% of population) — same five DataReportal pages as above
- DataReportal warns that data revisions mean figures are "not comparable with equivalent figures published in previous years" — [Digital 2023 India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2023-india)
- Internet users: 1.03 billion at end of 2025 (70.0% penetration) — [Digital 2026 India](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-india)
- YouTube Shorts had "650 million+ monthly logged-in viewers as of June 2025" in India; 85% of creators who upload on YouTube in India also upload Shorts; YouTube reaches 4 out of 5 internet users aged 18+ (Comscore); average daily YouTube consumption 72+ minutes among online adults — [FICCI-EY M&E Report, March 2026](https://aidcf.com/wp-content/uploads/FICCI-EY-Media-and-Entertainment-Report-2026_reduced.pdf)

**Discovery problem, both sides**
- 50.4% of creators cite "limited brand collaboration opportunities" as their main obstacle (Kofluence 2026, company survey) — [Storyboard18, 14 May 2026](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/46-creators-are-full-time-influencers-but-most-still-rely-on-side-income-streams-kofluence-report-98018.htm)
- 83% of marketers struggle with influencer discovery (95% in BFSI); 51% of marketers expect a rise of niche micro-influencers (Goat/Kantar India Influencer Marketing Report 2025) — [WPP Media, 10 June 2025](https://www.wppmedia.com/news/influencing-with-integrity); [MediaBrief, 24 June 2025](https://mediabrief.com/india-influencer-report-2025-goat-kantar-growth/)
- HashFame survey of "over 32,000 top creators": 55%+ lost brand deals because of no verified contact route or fake-manager representation; 62% were unaware brands had tried to reach them; 45% said unverified managers quoted inflated rates without authorisation; estimated ₹350 crore of collaborations lost in a year (company claim; method not disclosed) — [MediaBrief, 27 May 2025](https://mediabrief.com/indian-influencers-lost-over-%E2%82%B9350-cr-in-brand-deals-hashfame/)
- EY creator survey, indexed challenges (average = 100): building and growing a loyal audience 151; maintaining authenticity and credibility 147; finding and securing brand partnerships 142; staying up to date with trends 117; balancing content with other responsibilities 117; pressure to constantly produce 115; negative comments 73; legal and financial aspects 68; brand demands or conflicts 46 — [EY, "State of influencer marketing in India", April 2024, p.15](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/ey-state-of-influencer-marketing-in-india-03-04-2024.pdf)
- "Nearly three quarters of influencer marketing spends in India still flow directly between brands and creators, outside of any organised channel" (KlugKlug figure quoted in an op-ed by Pranav Panpalia, co-founder of OpraahFx; company claims) — [MediaNews4U, 4 April 2026](https://www.medianews4u.com/75-of-indias-influencer-marketing-happens-in-the-dark-thats-not-a-stat-thats-a-structural-failure/)
- Concentration: "90% of subscriptions from under 5% of channels" on YouTube (attributed to Citi by Kotak Mutual Fund; original not opened) — [Kotak MF blog, 27 November 2025](https://www.kotakmf.com/Information/blogs/inside-india-creator-economy)

**Engagement by tier (India)**
- EY (citing Brand Equity and BW, 2023): nano about 4%; micro about 2.5%; macro about 1.5%; mega about 2%. "Nano influencers had the highest engagement rate" — [EY, April 2024, p.9](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/ey-state-of-influencer-marketing-in-india-03-04-2024.pdf)
- Kofluence (company claim): nano 5–10%; micro 3–7%; macro (100K–1M) 1–3%; mega (1M+) 0.5–1.5% — [Kofluence blog, 22 July 2026](https://www.kofluence.com/blog/micro-vs-nano-vs-macro-influencers/)

**What small creators say**
- Aarja Bedi (38.6K followers, Delhi) used to get sponsored-content offers every few days and now gets "only a couple" a month — [The Nod Mag, 7 August 2026](https://thenodmag.com/content/indian-content-creator-influencer-economy-struggles)

- YouTube Hype launched in India in July 2025 for creators with 500 to 500,000 subscribers; viewers get three free hypes a week for videos in their first seven days; smaller channels get bonus points; the beta (Turkey, Taiwan, Brazil) recorded over 5 million hypes across 50,000+ channels in four weeks — [BuzzInContent, 15 July 2025](https://www.buzzincontent.com/news/youtube-launches-hype-feature-in-india-to-boost-small-creators-visibility-9498785)

- Meta's Edits Film Festival for India: submissions 24 August to 8 October 2026; top 10–20 Reels screened and showcased on @metaindia; jury includes Ahsaas Channa, Dolly Singh, Nikhil Taneja, Rajeev Masand; no cash prize stated — [Meta Newsroom, 24 August 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/08/introducing-the-edits-film-festival-to-spotlight-indias-emerging-creator-talent/)

### Inferences
- Instagram's Indian ad audience grew about 110% from early 2023 (229.6 million) to late 2025 (481 million) while the creator count grew about fourfold from 2020 to 2024; more creators are competing per viewer, which fits the "limited opportunities" complaint.
- The same gap is reported from both sides (creators cannot find deals; marketers cannot find creators; brands' messages never reach creators). That is direct Indian support for a discovery and verified-contact product.
- YouTube and Instagram both now run small-creator visibility programmes in India, which confirms the platforms themselves see under-discovery as a problem, and that their answer is leaderboards and contests rather than paid work.

### Gaps
- No reliable Indian data found on organic reach per post by follower tier (only engagement-rate benchmarks). Qoruz's 2025 benchmarks are global, not India-specific.
- No Indian survey quantifying small creators' reach decline. Blog posts claiming 30–80% reach drops in March–April 2026 were found but are unsourced and were not used.
- A DataReportal April 2026 figure of 513 million Instagram users in India appeared in a secondary site but was not opened.
- IAMAI/Kantar and RedSeer creator-side reach data were not found.

## 4. Brand deals in India

### Takeaway
Indian influencer-marketing spend is about ₹3,000–3,600 crore a year and growing about 20–25% annually, but a creator with under 10,000 followers typically gets ₹500–5,000 per Reel, and smaller creators are often offered products instead of money. Brands say they like small creators (47% prefer micro and nano; 52% allocation to micro), yet 71% pay a fixed fee with no standard rate card.

### Cited Findings

**Spend by year**

- The three estimates conflict for 2024: EY ₹2,344 crore versus Goat/Kantar ₹3,600 crore — [EY](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/ey-state-of-influencer-marketing-in-india-03-04-2024.pdf); contradicted by [WPP Media](https://www.wppmedia.com/news/influencing-with-integrity)

- At ₹500–5,000 per Reel (2025) a nano creator needs four to forty paid Reels a month to reach ₹20,000, while Kofluence's own data says about half of creators cite too few collaborations. Rates are not the only constraint; deal flow is.
- Rates quoted for the same tier differ by 10 to 40 times within one source (micro: ₹2,000 to ₹80,000), which supports the "no rate standard" complaint from both brands and creators.

### Gaps
- No reliable Indian data found on the share of total rupee spend going to nano and micro creators (only brand-preference percentages).
- No reliable Indian data found on the share of deals that are barter. A "35% barter-only" chart circulating online comes from a European agency blog with no source or India sample and was rejected.
- GroupM INCA, Qoruz (including a ₹700 crore festive-season estimate), Wobb, Zefmo and iCubesWire figures were not accessible (pages blocked or not found) and are not used.
- No count of the total number of Indian brands using creators was found; HUL's 12,000 creators is a single-company data point.

## 5. Payment problems in India

### Takeaway
No Indian survey measures what share of creators are paid late, but trade reporting says the standard brand payment cycle is 90 days and "regularly" stretches to as much as a year, and named creators report 3 to 18 month waits and write-offs. For small suppliers in general, the government's own MSME Samadhaan portal shows 2.57 lakh delayed-payment applications worth ₹55,244 crore. The law (45-day rule, compound interest) helps only those registered as micro enterprises.

### Cited Findings

**Creators and agencies**
- Official payment cycle for influencer campaigns is 90 days against a 45-day government norm for small businesses; delays run up to one year; intermediary agencies hold payments 90–120 days; brand audits every 15 days raise issues that take six to eight months to resolve; "none of them have been paid, which even extends for one year"; creators now demand a 25–50% advance before scripting or publishing; smaller agencies run on 6–8% margins; "it is usually the smaller clients that pay on time, not the bigger ones" (all sources anonymous) — [Storyboard18, 28 July 2025](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/payment-delays-plague-influencer-marketing-agencies-as-major-brands-lag-behind-77033.htm)
- Neha Tanti (101K followers, Gurgaon): about ₹8,00,000 in deals, ₹3,50,000 still pending; only one brand paid on time; one invoice chased for 1.5 years; "Every day, at 11 am, I message every brand I've worked with for money I have earned." — [The Nod Mag, 7 August 2026](https://thenodmag.com/content/indian-content-creator-influencer-economy-struggles)
- Sakshi Rawte-Dhar (54.6K, Goa): ₹2,20,000 owed for deals completed 3 to 9 months earlier; wrote off ₹20,000 from a beauty brand after a six-month fight. Aarja Bedi (38.6K, Delhi): ₹40,000 owed by an Indian protein brand for content posted a year ago — [The Nod Mag, 7 August 2026](https://thenodmag.com/content/indian-content-creator-influencer-economy-struggles)
- The article notes there are no standard contracts, employment norms or labour protections for creators, and that market saturation forces acceptance of unfair offers and silence about brand misconduct — [The Nod Mag, 7 August 2026](https://thenodmag.com/content/indian-content-creator-influencer-economy-struggles)
- "Creators are the backbone of today's marketing ecosystem, yet payment delays remain one of their biggest pain points" (Manjul Wadhwa, founder, Inflyx; company statement, no data) — [The Reel Stars, 23 March 2026](https://www.thereelstars.com/tech/indias-creator-economy-is-booming-but-broken-payments-are-becoming-its-biggest-bottleneck/)

**Musicians and performers**
- Karnataka's Department of Kannada and Culture (March 2025) had not paid over 1,800 artists, total dues above ₹4 crore, typically ₹20,000–25,000 per performer, some waiting 2–3 years despite a 15-day rule; one folk artist was owed ₹3.8 lakh for 19 performances since 2022 — [The Indian Music Diaries, Krishangi Sarma, 4 March 2026](https://theindianmusicdiaries.com/are-payment-delays-silencing-the-voices-of-independent-music/)
- Silheiba Ningombam (Meewakching): "we have encountered several instances where our performance fees were delayed, only partially paid, or not paid at all." A Pune musician describes labels handing bands unsold merchandise as payment adjustment — [The Indian Music Diaries, 4 March 2026](https://theindianmusicdiaries.com/are-payment-delays-silencing-the-voices-of-independent-music/)
- IPRS moved from 60 to 87 royalty distribution cycles in FY 2025-26 and pays monthly — [IPRS Annual Report 2025-26](https://iprs.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Annual_Report_FY2025-26.pdf)

**Freelancers and small suppliers generally**
- MSME Samadhaan, to 31 December 2025: 2,56,892 delayed-payment applications involving ₹55,244.31 crore; 24,238 settled mutually (₹3,018.37 crore); 52,744 yet to be examined (₹8,397.25 crore); 47,088 converted into cases (about ₹14,243 crore); 53,911 cases disposed by Facilitation Councils (₹14,638.38 crore). Economic Survey 2025-26 estimates ₹8.1 lakh crore locked in delayed payments to MSMEs — [SMEStreet, 29 July 2026](https://smestreet.in/smestreet-exclusive/msme-development-amendment-bill-2026-delayed-payments-analysis-12208060)
- "58% of freelancers have experienced not getting payments for their work" (Razorpay cites unnamed "studies"; unsourced, treat as weak) — [Razorpay Learn, 8 February 2024](https://razorpay.com/learn/scope-and-challenges-of-freelancers/)

**Legal remedies**
- MSMED Act 2006: Section 15 requires payment within the agreed period, not beyond 45 days from acceptance (15 days if there is no written agreement); Section 16 gives compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate, with monthly rests, automatically — [My Legal Pal](https://mylegalpal.com/articles/how-to-recover-payments-from-clients-in-india-msme-odr/); [SMEStreet, 29 July 2026](https://smestreet.in/smestreet-exclusive/msme-development-amendment-bill-2026-delayed-payments-analysis-12208060)
- The remedy is open only to Udyam-registered micro or small enterprises whose registration predates the invoice; the MSME ODR portal launched 27 June 2025 (free; negotiation then Facilitation Council arbitration; 90-day statutory decision period) — [My Legal Pal](https://mylegalpal.com/articles/how-to-recover-payments-from-clients-in-india-msme-odr/)
- The MSME ODR portal disposed of 17 cases in eight months (₹60.60 lakh recovered); the MSME Development (Amendment) Bill 2026 was introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 28 July 2026 — [SMEStreet, 29 July 2026](https://smestreet.in/smestreet-exclusive/msme-development-amendment-bill-2026-delayed-payments-analysis-12208060)
- Section 43B(h) of the Income Tax Act (from 1 April 2024) denies buyers a tax deduction for unpaid dues to micro and small suppliers until they pay — [My Legal Pal](https://mylegalpal.com/articles/how-to-recover-payments-from-clients-in-india-msme-odr/)
- The new Labour Codes recognise gig and platform workers for social security, and FICCI-EY notes that "content creators operate outside of conventional studio systems" and that performers and influencers engaged "under various contractual labels" will be classified by the substance of the relationship — [FICCI-EY M&E Report, March 2026](https://aidcf.com/wp-content/uploads/FICCI-EY-Media-and-Entertainment-Report-2026_reduced.pdf)

### Inferences
- The legal remedy exists but is mismatched to creators: it requires prior Udyam registration, and only 15.2% of creators are registered business entities or GST individuals (Kofluence 2026). Most small creators and artists therefore have only a legal notice or civil suit, which costs more than a typical ₹5,000–40,000 invoice.
- The average Samadhaan claim is about ₹21.5 lakh (₹55,244 crore over 2,56,892 applications), far above creator invoice sizes, which suggests the portal is used by larger suppliers rather than individual freelancers.
- Indian creators are effectively giving brands 90 days to a year of free credit; the industry's own response (25–50% advances) shows escrow or milestone payment is already accepted practice at the top end.

### Gaps
- No reliable Indian data found on the percentage of creators, artists or freelancers paid late or not paid, nor on average days of delay. There is no Indian equivalent of the US or UK freelancer late-payment surveys. A "one in three influencers report delayed payments" line appeared in a search summary but could not be found on the page and is not used.
- Refrens, Recordent and Razorpay publish no usable primary data on freelancer payment delays (Razorpay's 58% is unsourced).
- No Indian statute comparable to New York's Freelance Isn't Free Act was found.
- All Storyboard18 payment-delay sources are anonymous; there are no named brands.

## 6. Scams in India

### Takeaway
Indians reported about ₹22,500 crore of cyber-fraud losses in each of 2024 and 2025, roughly ten times the 2022 level, and I4C named work-from-home and part-time job scams the most-reported cybercrime of 2023. There is no official count of fake brand-collaboration or fake casting scams, but police cases show the pattern: fake casting agents on Instagram and WhatsApp collecting ₹1,000 to ₹75,000 per victim, and one creator extorted of ₹50 lakh through fake copyright strikes.

### Cited Findings

**Official cyber-fraud figures**
- Losses reported by citizens: ₹7,465.18 crore (2023); ₹22,845.73 crore (2024), up almost 206% (Minister of State for Home Bandi Sanjay Kumar, Lok Sabha written reply, 22 July 2025; NCRP and CFCFRMS data) — [Inc42, 22 July 2025](https://inc42.com/buzz/indians-lost-inr-22845-cr-to-cyber-fraud-in-2024-govt/)

- Fake managers: 55%+ of surveyed creators lost deals to unverified contact routes or fake-manager representation; 45% said unverified managers quoted inflated rates (example: a ₹5 lakh deal quoted at ₹10 lakh); ₹350 crore estimated lost (HashFame, company claim) — [MediaBrief, 27 May 2025](https://mediabrief.com/indian-influencers-lost-over-%E2%82%B9350-cr-in-brand-deals-hashfame/)

### Inferences
- Reported cyber-fraud losses rose about tenfold from 2022 (₹2,290 crore) to 2024 (₹22,845 crore) and stayed flat in 2025 while case counts rose 24%, so the average loss per case fell; scams are getting broader and smaller-ticket, which is the profile of casting and collaboration scams (₹1,000 to ₹75,000 per victim).
- Only about 2% of 2025 complaints became FIRs (55,484 of 28.15 lakh), so small-ticket victims such as aspiring artists rarely get police action. Prevention (verified identities, verified brands) matters more than recourse.
- The casting cases share one mechanism: an unverifiable identity on Instagram or WhatsApp plus an upfront fee (portfolio, flight, registration). A verified-opportunity feed with a "never pay to audition" rule addresses it directly.

### Gaps
- No reliable Indian data found on the number or value of fake brand-collaboration, fake casting or fake modelling scams; NCRB and I4C do not publish that category. Evidence is case-level only.
- A figure of 11,126 "online job fraud" incidents in 2025 (Rajya Sabha reply, March 2026) appeared in a search summary but was not present on the page when opened; unverified and not used.
- 2021 loss figure not verified. NCRB "Crime in India" tables were not opened.
- No police case specifically involving a fake brand-PR "collaboration" phishing of an Indian creator was found in accessible press; only security-vendor blogs describe it.

## 7. AI and Indian artists

### Takeaway
There is no Indian survey of artists' losses to AI, but there is strong institutional evidence: a government committee has proposed that AI firms may train on all lawfully accessed Indian works under a mandatory licence with royalties and no opt-out for creators; Indian music labels have gone to court against OpenAI; and working Indian illustrators and musicians report clients using AI as leverage to cut fees.

### Cited Findings
- DPIIT committee Working Paper on Generative AI and Copyright (Part 1, December 2025): by majority it recommends "a mandatory blanket license in favour of AI Developers for the use of all lawfully accessed copyright-protected works in the training of AI Systems, accompanied by a statutory remuneration right"; "the rights holders will not have the option to withhold their works"; royalties (a percentage of revenue from AI systems) would be collected by a central non-profit body (CRCAT) and paid via copyright societies to members and registered non-members; rates set by a government-appointed committee. Nasscom dissented, favouring a text-and-data-mining exception with opt-out — [DPIIT Working Paper, December 2025](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/ff266bbeed10c48e3479c941484f3525.pdf)
- The same paper rejects an opt-out model because it "leaves small creators largely unprotected owing to lack of awareness to opt-out, bargaining power to negotiate, and the mechanisms to see if their content has been scraped despite opt-out" and "structurally favour[s] large players over individual creators" — [DPIIT Working Paper, December 2025](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/ff266bbeed10c48e3479c941484f3525.pdf)
- ANI v. OpenAI (Delhi High Court, filed 2024): in February 2025 the Indian Music Industry, T-Series and Saregama (with Sony Music also reported) sought to join, alleging "unauthorised use of sound recordings" to train AI; NDTV, Indian Express, Hindustan Times and the Digital News Publishers Association also sought to join — [Business Today, 14 February 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/artificial-intelligence/story/why-t-series-saregama-sony-want-to-join-a-copyright-lawsuit-against-openai-in-india-464747-2025-02-14)
- Arijit Singh v. Codible Ventures LLP (spelt "Codabile" in the report): the court held that an AI tool letting users substitute a celebrity's voice was a violation of personality rights; Jackie Shroff v. The Peppy Store restrained AI deepfake use of likeness and voice. FICCI-EY advises artists to ensure contracts do not let producers or labels use their attributes "for training or developing AI" — [FICCI-EY M&E Report, March 2026](https://aidcf.com/wp-content/uploads/FICCI-EY-Media-and-Entertainment-Report-2026_reduced.pdf)
- IPRS (Javed Akhtar, chairman; Rakesh Nigam, CEO) backs CISAC's call for transparency in AI training data, mandatory authorisation and fair compensation; CISAC projects music creators could lose up to 24% of revenues by 2028 (global figure, no India-specific estimate) — [MediaBrief, 30 May 2025](https://mediabrief.com/iprs-cisac-2025-royalty-growth-ai-regulation/)
- Animator and illustrator Nayanika Chatterjee says clients suggest using AI to cut costs, which she treats as a red flag; rapper and jingle writer Naveen Koomar stopped making music for months after AI displaced his work and says clients used AI as negotiating leverage on fees — [The Established, Takshi Mehta, 21 April 2025](https://www.theestablished.com/culture/living/who-can-really-claim-to-be-an-artist-when-the-medium-is-a-machine)
- Artist Viraj Khanna: "I've already seen elements of my own work being lifted. Faces and sculptural details I've made show up in others' clothing and designs." The film Raanjhanaa was re-released in August 2025 with an AI-altered ending without the director's consent — [The Nod Mag, Sheya Kurian, 19 December 2025](https://thenodmag.com/content/artificial-intelligence-creative-industry-backlash)
- 3D artist ROHO calls AI training "a fully unethical scheme ... distilling millions of collective hours spent by artists into vending machine levels of easy 'creation'"; 3D artist Jishnu: "I ... had multiple instances of my work plagiarised, I understand how important consent and ownership are." — [Homegrown, Pari Pradhan, 5 August 2025](https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-voices/we-spoke-to-5-artists-about-generative-ais-place-in-the-indian-creative-landscape)
- Creators themselves use AI: 59% of creators use AI tools regularly or sometimes (64.4% for ideation, 31.9% for creative design), 17.3% never (Kofluence 2026, company survey) — [MediaNews4U, 15 May 2026](https://www.medianews4u.com/15-of-indias-creators-are-now-registered-business-entities-as-influencer-marketing-crosses-a-structural-threshold-kofluence/); Kofluence's CEO earlier said 76% of creators use AI — [IBTimes India, 8 July 2025](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/how-much-do-creators-really-earn-kofluence-ceo-decodes-indias-influencer-economy-interview-885077)
- 29–30% of brands have adopted generative AI for creative development (Kofluence 2025, company claim) — [Storyboard18, 8 July 2025](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/creator-economy-in-india-hits-rs-3500-crore-fueled-by-ecomm-fmcg-sectors-kofluence-73457.htm)

### Inferences
- If the DPIIT majority view becomes law, Indian artists will not be able to keep their work out of AI training; their only lever will be registering works to receive royalties. A platform that records authorship and dates for artists' work would have practical value under that regime.
- The Indian AI debate so far is led by music labels and news publishers; individual visual artists and illustrators have no collecting society or representative body in these proceedings.

### Gaps
- No reliable Indian data found: no survey gives the percentage of Indian artists, illustrators or musicians who have lost work or income to AI.
- No Indian source was found on whether Indian Instagram users can opt out of Meta's AI training; international coverage says there is no opt-out outside the EU and UK, but that is not India-specific evidence.
- No Indian lawsuit by an individual visual artist over AI training was found.

## 8. What Indian artists say about finding gigs and clients

### Takeaway
Indian artists find work through Instagram, personal networks, WhatsApp and Facebook groups, event agencies and a few booking marketplaces, with no agents, rate cards or standard contracts. Their recurring complaints are too few paid opportunities, "exposure" or barter instead of money, chasing payments alone, and visibility controlled by gatekeepers.

### Cited Findings
- Channels listed by an Indian booking platform (company blog): Instagram, YouTube and LinkedIn for visibility and DMs to past clients; café and lounge managers and open mics; wedding planners and corporate event agencies; "Facebook groups and WhatsApp networks for regional artist circles"; booking platforms (StarClinch, Gigstart, BookMyTalent) — [StarClinch blog, 16 December 2025](https://starclinch.com/blog/how-to-find-live-music-gigs-in-india-complete-guide-for-emerging-artists/)
- Illustrators: "India has zero illustration agencies"; illustrators negotiate their own contracts, vet their own IP clauses and chase their own payments; most independent publishers have no art director; no India-specific rate guide exists. "I still had to personally catch that the termination clause in this agreement was structured to disadvantage me." — [Aparajitha Vaasudev, Substack, 19 August 2026](https://studioapara.substack.com/p/india-has-no-illustration-agents)
- Musicians: fees "delayed, only partially paid, or not paid at all" (Silheiba Ningombam); "There's a growing need for structured systems that ensure artists are compensated on time" (Guwahati musician) — [The Indian Music Diaries, 4 March 2026](https://theindianmusicdiaries.com/are-payment-delays-silencing-the-voices-of-independent-music/)
- Musicians: club gigs pay a "token fee"; costs of flights "have almost doubled" post-Covid; everyone interviewed keeps side work (2022) — [Rolling Stone India, 13 July 2022](https://rollingstoneindia.com/heres-what-its-really-like-to-be-an-indian-indie-artist-playing-live-gigs/)
- Musicians: opening acts "rehearse, travel, and perform" for little or nothing "in the promise of exposure"; session musicians often get neither royalty nor credit — [Rolling Stone India, 12 September 2025](https://rollingstoneindia.com/what-the-music-industry-doesnt-talk-about-enough-minimum-wage-for-musicians)
- Musicians: recording, marketing and touring costs "are often prohibitive"; survival relies on session work for Bollywood, teaching, ad jingles and commercial gigs; artists in smaller states lack venues and studios — [The Indian Music Diaries, 26 September 2025](https://theindianmusicdiaries.com/who-gets-to-stay-indie-the-struggles-behind-the-indian-independent-music-scene/)
- Creators: 50.4% say limited brand-collaboration opportunities are the main obstacle (Kofluence 2026) — [Storyboard18, 14 May 2026](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/46-creators-are-full-time-influencers-but-most-still-rely-on-side-income-streams-kofluence-report-98018.htm)
- Creators: Aarja Bedi "didn't start in the era of the fancy content creator"; Sakshi Rawte-Dhar spends ₹6,000 on subtitling software and would need ₹20,000 a month for an editor; creators stay silent on brand misconduct for fear of losing work — [The Nod Mag, 7 August 2026](https://thenodmag.com/content/indian-content-creator-influencer-economy-struggles)
- Music creators: 73% strongly feel they have much to learn about production and 56% about monetisation; only 56% have the equipment they need — [EY, "The music creator economy", December 2023](https://iprs.org/wp-content/uploads/ey-the-music-creator-economy.pdf)
- Live events are growing: organised live-events revenue rose 44% to ₹145 billion in 2025 (₹73 bn in 2022, ₹88 bn in 2023, ₹101 bn in 2024); over 130 concert days with audiences above 10,000 in 2025 — [FICCI-EY M&E Report, March 2026](https://aidcf.com/wp-content/uploads/FICCI-EY-Media-and-Entertainment-Report-2026_reduced.pdf)

### Inferences
- The live-events market doubled in three years (₹73 bn to ₹145 bn) while small acts still report token fees and non-payment; money is entering the sector but not reaching the bottom tier, which is a distribution and matching failure rather than a lack of demand.
- Across musicians, illustrators and creators the missing pieces are the same four: a way to be found, a going rate, a contract, and someone to chase payment. Those map directly to Underdawg features.

### Gaps
- No reliable Indian data found on what share of gigs or commissions come through Instagram DMs, WhatsApp groups or agencies; evidence is anecdotal or from company blogs.
- No Indian reporting specific to how dancers, photographers or visual artists find clients was found in accessible sources.
- Reddit and other forum discussions by Indian artists were not searched within the search budget.

# Feature 7 "Art Shop": how independent artists sell originals, prints, handmade work and printed products online (global, extra India depth)

Researcher: r4. Research date: 2026-10-01. Scope: validate or disprove Underdawg's "Art Shop" (artist lists any physical art; self-ship via aggregator or own courier with escrow release after delivery; optional print-on-demand (POD); AI room/product mockups; buyer protection with tracking, damage/return policy, disputes and certificate of authenticity (COA); sold via the link-in-bio Shop block and Instagram DMs; % commission).

Reddit served a block page to every request, so **I could not collect three or more independent Reddit threads for artist-side complaints about shipping damage, DM scams or POD copycats.** Those items are listed as gaps. I did not invent them.
- Labels: **[Verified]** = I opened the page and the number is on it. **[Reported]** = a third party or the platform itself claims it and I could not check it independently. **[Inference]** = my analysis, not a sourced fact.

## (A) Problem validation: do independent/emerging artists sell art online, through which channels, how much income does it give them, and what are their pains?

### Takeaway
Demand is real and moving to artist-direct and Instagram-led buying. Artist-direct sales (studio, Instagram, commissions) doubled from 10% to 20% of high-net-worth collectors' art spending between 2021 and 2025. In 2023, 29% of online art buyers (42% of under-35s) had bought art directly through Instagram. The established ways to sell work against emerging artists, though: open marketplaces are saturated and increasingly ad-driven or curated, curated galleries take 40–50% plus subscriptions, and POD platforms are cutting small artists' earnings or removing them. The core unsolved problem is not "a place to list art". It is turning an artist's own social attention into a trusted, low-friction, low-fee transaction (payment trust, shipping, returns, authenticity). It is not discovery.

### Cited Findings

**Channels and demand (buyer side)**
- [Verified] Arts Economics' 2025 survey of high-net-worth (HNW) collectors: the share of spending on artist-direct sales ("including studio sales, Instagram-based purchases, and direct commissions") doubled from 10% in 2021 to 20% in 2025. Dealers still took 43% of spending. — [Art Basel & UBS Art Market Report 2026, p.77 (published March 2026)](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf)
- [Verified] 20% of HNW collectors preferred buying from artists, up from 6% in 2024. **42% of collectors active for two years or less preferred to buy direct from an artist.** — [Art Basel & UBS 2026, p.77](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf)
- [Verified] Dealers quoted in the report: "Our biggest issue by far in 2025 is artists selling directly to customers who find the artist in our gallery." The report links rising disintermediation to more "direct contact from collectors with artists via Instagram and other social networks". — [Art Basel & UBS 2026, p.77](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf)
- [Verified] 74% of online art buyers use Instagram for art (84% of young buyers). In 2023, **29% had bought art directly through Instagram** (21% in 2022), and **42% of buyers under 35** had done so in the past 12 months (24% in 2022). 42% said social media influenced their purchase, as did 52% of new buyers. — [Hiscox Online Art Trade Report 2023, pp.18–19 (April 2023)](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2023-04/Hiscox%20online%20art%20trade%20report%202023.pdf)
- [Verified] Counterpoint: the Art Basel report cites research that "direct-selling online platforms that emerged in the last decade often simply replicated the functions of existing offline structures, creating new intermediaries rather than replacing them". The cited paper is Ricucci & Blank (2025), "The Limits of Platforms: Why Disintermediation has Failed in the Art Market", *New Media & Society*. — [Art Basel & UBS 2026, pp.76–77](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf) (the paper itself was paywalled and not opened)

**Income (artist side)**
- [Verified, 2018 data] A survey of 1,016 visual artists (spring 2018) found almost half earned just 0–10% of their income from their art practice, and only 17% earned 75–100% from artwork. Only 12% listed gallery sales as a top-three income source, while 61% named freelance/contract work. The median income was $20–30K. — [The Creative Independent, "A study on the financial state of visual artists today" (2018)](https://thecreativeindependent.com/artist-survey/)
- [Verified] Etsy's Seller Census: 89% of Etsy sellers are "businesses of one", and about half sell only on Etsy. — [Etsy Inc. Form 10-K FY2025 (filed 2026-02-19)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)

**Pain 1: high take rates on art-specific platforms**
- [Verified] Saatchi Art takes 40% of the final sale price of an original. Promotional discounts are split with the artist, and the artist pays for packaging. — [Saatchi Art "Why sell" (Wayback snapshot 2026-09-07)](https://www.saatchiart.com/whysell). Conflict: an older, undated review says 35%: [Very Private Gallery](https://veryprivategallery.com/what-is-saatchi-art/). Treat 40% as current.
- [Verified] Artfinder charges 45% commission plus £10/€12/$14 a month on its Core plan, or 40% plus £30/€35/$40 a month on Premium. UK sellers also pay VAT on the commission. — [Artfinder seller plans (snapshot 2026-06-16)](https://sellers.artfinder.com/article/927-what-are-artfinders-seller-plan-subscriptions); [Artfinder "How much will I be paid?" (updated 2026-03-16)](https://sellers.artfinder.com/article/481-how-much-will-i-be-paid)
- [Reported by an artist] Singulart "already takes the highest commission of 50%" and added monthly subscriptions of €29.99, €69.99 and €149.99. The artist called the top plan's analytics "useless" and said her emails to support went unanswered. — [Threads @saravossart, 2024-09-10 (post 1)](https://www.threads.com/@saravossart/post/C_urzgZONVj); [post 2](https://www.threads.com/@saravossart/post/C_uscqLOj7U). Singulart's official pricing page returned HTTP 405 and was not verified.
- [Verified] Singulart scores 1.7/5 from 10 reviews on SmartCustomer. Reviews from February and October 2025 include artists calling it a scam and complaining that money was held during "investigations" for months. — [SmartCustomer: Singulart](https://www.smartcustomer.com/reviews/singulart.com) (small sample; low weight)
- [Verified, but the source is a competitor] Solene Haus (2026) puts the annual cost of commission at Saatchi (40%), Artfinder (40–45% plus subscription) and galleries (40–60%, sometimes with $3,000–10,000 upfront fees). It describes Etsy as roughly 9–11% in total but "heavily algorithm-driven toward established sellers". — [Solene Haus blog, 2026-06-07](https://www.solenehaus.com/blog/art-platform-commission-comparison-2026)
- [Verified] Bring-your-own-audience problem: on Saatchi, "if you bring your collectors to Saatchi Art, you are still charged 35% commissions… You risk losing your collectors to other artists on the same platform." — [Very Private Gallery review (undated)](https://veryprivategallery.com/what-is-saatchi-art/)

**Pain 2: POD platforms are deteriorating for small artists. This is a common complaint, backed by 4 independent sources.**
- [Verified] Society6 removed many artist accounts on 2025-03-18 to "operate as a more focused brand with a smaller pool of artists". An artist with 10+ years on POD platforms says most artists she heard from were picked for removal, and suggests AI-generated and low-quality uploads were a cause. — [CatCoq, Feb 2025](https://catcoq.com/blog/society6-shutting-down-artist-accounts)
- [Verified] The Creator's Diary quotes Reddit users who received rejection and acceptance emails minutes apart. It also notes earlier Society6 moves (paid artist plans, shipping fees deducted from earnings). — [The Creator's Diary (2025)](https://thecreatorsdiary.com/creator-business/society6-removing-artist-profiles-pod-alternatives/)
- [Verified] Society6 now pays a fixed 5–10% markup over base price: 10% on pillows, tapestries, framed art and posters, 5% on everything else. Artists can no longer set their own markups. — [Society6 Pricing & Earnings (snapshot 2026-04-15)](https://help.society6.com/en-US/articles/pricing-earnings-258221). In December 2025 Society6 also removed artist "About" pages and design listing pages, leaving one shoppable collection page per artist. — [Society6 update, 29 Dec 2025 (snapshot 2026-04-15)](https://help.society6.com/en-US/updates-to-society6-artists-account---december-29-2025-3897034)
- [Verified] A small artist on Redbubble made 8 sales between 2022 and July 2025 and "never enough to actually get paid out". She concluded that under the Standard tier fees "you'll almost certainly earn next to nothing", e.g. needing 42 sticker sales a month just to cover an $8.40 minimum account fee. — [inspiremari.nl, 2025-07-07](https://inspiremari.nl/redbubble-is-a-horrible-platform-for-small-artists/). An earlier account-fee change (2023) was criticised by [Top Bubble Index](https://www.topbubbleindex.com/blog/redbubble-recent-change-impact/).

**Pain 3: saturation and discoverability inside marketplaces (multiple independent sources)**
- [Verified] Etsy grew revenue in 2025 mainly through Etsy Ads, including "more seamless integration of onsite ads within organic search results". Ad revenue rose by $62.2M, "largely driven by an increase in average price per click". Etsy says "our top buyers and sellers drive the majority of Etsy's GMS". — [Etsy 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)
- [Verified] Saatchi is described as "very competitive and saturated", citing about 110,000 artists from Wikipedia: "If you are not getting likes and shares on Instagram or YouTube, you will likely not get discovered on Saatchi Art." — [Very Private Gallery](https://veryprivategallery.com/what-is-saatchi-art/)
- [Verified] Singulart, which is curated, had about 12,000 artists and sold about 1,700 artworks a month in 2025. — [Wikipedia: Singulart (accessed 2026-10-01)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singulart)

**Pain 4: trust, shipping, returns, authenticity (buyer side, which limits artist sales)**
- [Verified] Among people who had not bought art online in 2023, the barriers were: the work may look different in real life (70%), cannot inspect condition (66%), seller reputation (63%), authenticity (52%), returns (50%), shipping and packaging (42%), discomfort paying larger sums online (39%) and insurance (38%). — [Hiscox 2023, p.15](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2023-04/Hiscox%20online%20art%20trade%20report%202023.pdf)
- [Verified, 2020 data] 11% of online art buyers said a purchase was damaged in transit, down from 31% in 2019. 56% had been put off a purchase by a site's return policy (62% of new buyers). Of platforms offering returns, 77% made the client pay return shipping. — [Hiscox Online Art Trade Report 2020 Part 3](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2021-04/hoatr_report_2020_part3.pdf)

**Pain 5: copycats, IP theft and AI-generated art**
- [Verified] Etsy says it "regularly receive[s] and expect[s] to continue receiving claims alleging that items listed by sellers… are counterfeit, infringing". — [Etsy 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)
- [Verified] Society6's curation reset is linked by one artist to AI-generated and low-quality uploads. — [CatCoq](https://catcoq.com/blog/society6-shutting-down-artist-accounts)

### Inferences
- **Problem being solved** [Inference]: Artists are already selling through Instagram, but checkout is manual (DM back-and-forth, UPI screenshots, no buyer protection, no shipping workflow, no COA). Where a structured channel exists, it either takes 40–50% (curated online galleries) or pays a thin 5–10% royalty on POD with poor visibility.
- **Severity**: Medium-High for artists who already have some audience and sell physical work. Low for artists with no audience, because their problem is demand, not checkout.
- **Frequency**: Occasional. Most emerging artists sell irregularly. Etsy buyers purchase about 3 times a year on average ([Etsy 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)), and art is a lower-frequency purchase than general Etsy items [Inference].
- **Segments hit hardest**: painters, illustrators and photographers selling originals or prints; craft and handmade makers; fashion creatives selling one-off pieces. Musicians are a merch-only case already served by Bandcamp and Fourthwall.
- **Current workaround**: Instagram DM + UPI/bank transfer + manual courier booking; Etsy for exports (India); link-in-bio stores (Linktree/Beacons at 9% on free plans); POD storefronts (Blinkstore/Qikink/Printrove). See section B.
- **Why existing options fall short**: high commission when the artist brings the buyer; no buyer protection on DM sales; POD platforms de-prioritising small artists; Etsy India historically export-only (section J).
- **Disproof check** [Inference]: "If this feature disappeared, would artists care?" Artists who sell would care about checkout, shipping and trust tooling. Artists who don't yet sell would not miss it. The feature does not create the audience; Instagram does.

### Gaps
- No reliable public data on what share of independent artists' income comes from online sales, globally or in India. The Creative Independent figure covers all art-practice income (2018).
- Artist-side complaints about shipping damage, DM payment scams (overpayment/"shipping agent" scams) and POD copycat theft could not be verified with 3+ independent discussions, because Reddit was blocked and the search budget was exhausted. Do not call these "common complaints" without further research.
- No India-specific survey found on how Indian artists sell (Instagram DM vs marketplaces).

## Market data: online art sales size and share, buyer segments, India market

### Takeaway
Online art is a large but shrinking minority channel: $9.2B in 2025, 15% of a $59.6B market. It is concentrated at lower price points, which is where emerging artists sell. New and young buyers lean on Instagram and artist-direct buying. India's measured art market is small (about $144M in 2023, mostly auction turnover) and growing fastest in a ₹5–25 lakh bracket that is above most emerging artists' prices.

### Cited Findings
- [Verified] The global art market was $59.6B in 2025 (+4%) across 41.5M transactions. Online sales were $9.2B, "lowest level since 2019", and the online-only share fell to 15%, from a peak of 25% in 2020. Online sales were $13.3B (2021 peak), $11.0B (2022) and $10.5B (2024). — [Art Basel & UBS 2026: Global sales page](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/global-sales); [full report pp.24–26](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf)
- [Verified] Online sits at the low end: 63% of online-only auction value in 2025 came from lots under $50,000, versus 13% of offline value. The $5,000–50,000 band made up 45% of online auction value. Lots over $1M were 2% of online value versus 56% offline. — [Art Basel & UBS 2026](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf)
- [Verified] Online was 16% of dealer turnover in 2025 (down 6 points). Dealers' own websites and digital channels made up 14%, while **third-party online platforms were only 2% of dealer sales, the lowest in six years**. 40% of dealers' online sales by value went to new buyers. — [Art Basel & UBS 2026, pp.~118–123](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf)
- [Verified] 49% of the buyers dealers sold to in 2025 were new to their business. For the smallest dealers it was 60%. "Many first-time collectors start off at lower price points through smaller galleries." — [Art Basel & UBS 2026, p.~98](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf)
- [Verified] Hiscox estimated online art sales at $10.8B in 2022 (up from $10.2B in 2021). This differs from Art Basel's $11.0B for 2022 because of methodology. The 2023 edition is the latest Hiscox Online Art Trade Report on its archive page. — [Hiscox archive](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/online-art-trade-report/archive); [Hiscox 2023](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2023-04/Hiscox%20online%20art%20trade%20report%202023.pdf)
- [Verified, older finding] The Hiscox 2023 timeline restates its 2016–17 finding that "online art sales are dominated by pieces priced below $5,000". — [Hiscox 2023, p.6](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2023-04/Hiscox%20online%20art%20trade%20report%202023.pdf)
- [Verified] India's art market turnover was $144.3M (about ₹1,253 crore) in 2023, per the "State of the Indian Art Market Report". "Most robust growth" is in the ₹5–25 lakh bracket, favoured by younger and first-time collectors, and collectors increasingly come from smaller cities. — [Business Standard, Veenu Sandhu, 2025-09-24](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/gst-rate-cut-from-12-to-5-poised-to-brighten-india-s-art-landscape-125092400909_1.html). IBEF gives ₹1,279 crore for the same $144.3M (FY23 report); the difference is currency conversion. — [IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/news/gst-rate-cut-from-12-to-5-poised-to-brighten-india-s-art-landscape)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Underdawg's price band (emerging artists, roughly ₹1,000–₹50,000) is the segment where online and social buying is most accepted, so the channel fit is good. The measured "Indian art market" ($144M) is mostly auction and gallery value and is not the addressable market for an emerging-artist shop.
- [Inference] Third-party platforms are only 2% of dealer sales and HNW buyers increasingly buy direct from artists. Value is shifting to artist-owned channels (own site, Instagram), which supports building checkout tooling for the artist's own audience rather than a destination marketplace.

### Gaps
- No reliable public data on the size of India's online art or handmade-craft e-commerce market, or on the value of art sold via Instagram in India.
- The Hiscox Online Art Trade Report has had no edition since 2023, so no Hiscox buyer data is available for 2024–2026.

## (B) Competitors globally and in India: implementations, fees and adoption

### Takeaway
"Sell your art online" is a very crowded category. It spans curated online galleries (40–50% commission), open marketplaces (about 10% fees plus ads), POD royalty platforms (5–10% markups, shrinking), store builders and link-in-bio shops (0–9% plus subscriptions), and Indian POD/storefront players (free, earning on base price, with COD). No single competitor found combines Instagram DM selling, escrow with self-ship booking, POD for prints, AI room mockups and COA for India. But each piece exists, and the cheapest path (DM + UPI) is free.

## Buyer trust and conversion: COAs, view-in-room/AR, AI mockups

### Takeaway
Buyers say condition reports, COAs, provenance, 30-day returns, shipping and packing information and insurance would make them more confident buying art online. These are the levers Underdawg's "buyer protection" bundle targets. AR "View in Room" (Saatchi) and AI in-context images (Artfinder) are already standard on art marketplaces, so AI mockups are table stakes, not a differentiator. No reliable public data quantifies how much mockups or AR lift art sales.

### Cited Findings
- [Verified] Share of hesitant online art buyers in 2023 saying each service would increase their confidence: condition report 91%, provenance tracking 84%, **COA 78%**, more background on the artist or object 78%, **30-day return guarantee 75%**, **more information on shipping and packing 72%**, option to insure 69%, pricing tool 58%, independent valuation 58%, talk to an expert 57%, expert endorsement 53%, interest-free repayment plan 37%, celebrity endorsement 8%. — [Hiscox 2023, p.16](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2023-04/Hiscox%20online%20art%20trade%20report%202023.pdf)
- [Verified] 79% of new art buyers would be more confident if they could talk to an advisor. 90% said tracking an artwork's history and provenance would increase confidence. — [Hiscox 2023, p.16](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2023-04/Hiscox%20online%20art%20trade%20report%202023.pdf)
- [Verified, 2020] 70% of hesitant buyers would be more likely to buy if shipping were free, but only 10% of platforms offered it. — [Hiscox 2020 Part 3](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2021-04/hoatr_report_2020_part3.pdf)
- [Verified] Saatchi Art: "Using the power of augmented reality, collectors can take advantage of our 'View in Room' feature"; up to 5 images per artwork. — [Saatchi whysell](https://www.saatchiart.com/whysell)
- [Verified] Artfinder includes "Generate in context images" in both seller plans. — [Artfinder plans](https://sellers.artfinder.com/article/927-what-are-artfinders-seller-plan-subscriptions)
- [Verified] Artflute: "Every piece comes with a certificate of authenticity." — [Artflute](https://www.artflute.com/)
- [Verified] Mockup tools: Mockey.ai is a "Free AI Mockup Generator with 30,000+ Mockup Templates", claims "1M+ mockups created" and "50K+ professionals" (self-reported). — [Mockey](https://mockey.ai/). Printful's free plan includes a free Mockup Generator. — [Printful pricing](https://www.printful.com/pricing). Placeit sells mockup templates, including "AI-Made Mockups"; plan prices were not captured. — [Placeit plans](https://placeit.net/plans)

### Inferences
- [Inference] The biggest stated barrier is "looks different in real life" (70%). AI wall mockups help with scale and context but can worsen colour and texture mismatch, and AI mockups that misrepresent the work could increase "not as described" disputes. Rule: real photos (front, angle, close-up of texture, edge or side) are mandatory, and mockups are labelled "visualisation" and drawn at true scale from the declared dimensions.
- [Inference] A lightweight digital COA (unique ID, artist signature, QR link to the Underdawg listing and sale record) is cheap to build. It answers the 78% COA demand, and the verified sale record doubles as credibility data for the artist.

### Gaps
- No reliable public data found on conversion uplift from AR "view in room" or AI mockups for art specifically.
- No adoption metrics found for Canva/Smartmockups or Placeit, as I could not search.

## (C) Is the feature unique? Classification and dimensions

### Takeaway
At feature level, selling art online is **Very common**. The specific India-first combination (Instagram DM-triggered checkout plus escrow plus artist self-ship via an aggregator plus POD plus COA plus AI mockups in an artist link-in-bio) is **Emerging/Rare**, but it is a bundle of commodity parts with low defensibility.

### Cited Findings
- Functional parity exists. Marketplaces for originals: Saatchi, Artfinder, Singulart, Mojarto, Artflute. Link-in-bio selling: Linktree and Beacons (9% seller fee on free plans). POD storefronts: Blinkstore, Qikink, Fourthwall. Managed shipping: Saatchi (courier pickup), Artfinder (Arta). AI in-context images: Artfinder. COA: Artflute. All sources are in the section B table.
- Etsy explicitly prohibits moving transactions off Etsy, the opposite of an artist-owned-audience model. — [Etsy fees policy](https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees/)

### Inferences
- **Classification: Very common** for "artists sell physical art online". **Common** for artist-specific audiences (Saatchi, Artfinder, Singulart, INPRNT, Society6, Redbubble all target artists). **Emerging** for "social-DM-native checkout for artists in India".
- **Functional uniqueness: Low.** Every function exists somewhere.
- **Audience uniqueness: Low-Moderate.** Artist-only selling platforms are common. Under-discovered Indian artists selling originals through their own Instagram, with India-native logistics (COD/RTO-aware, Shiprocket) and GST-aware flows, is less served. Blinkstore and Qikink serve merch, not originals.
- **Workflow uniqueness: Moderate.** Comment/DM → automated checkout link → escrow → artist books courier from the app → tracked delivery → release → auto-COA. No single India product found doing this end to end, but Instamojo plus Shiprocket plus manual DMs approximates it.
- **Network uniqueness: Low.** Buyers are not part of an artists-only network. Unless Underdawg adds collectors, brands or curators as a buyer side, the network adds little to the shop. It could help through artist-to-artist cross-promotion ("artists you may also like" on checkout and thank-you pages).
- **Data uniqueness: Moderate-High (potential).** Verified sales, prices, repeat buyers, city or price-band demand and on-time dispatch build a verified track record (credibility) that portfolio sites lack. This could feed reputation and curator and brand discovery, which is Underdawg's core goal.
- **Discovery uniqueness: Low by default.** A shop mostly monetises existing attention (section E). It only becomes a discovery mechanism if shop items feed a fair-exposure buyer surface.
- **Combination uniqueness: Moderate.** The combination is rare in India, but each piece is replicable by Linktree, Instagram or Instamojo. Defensibility would come from the credibility data, not the checkout.

### Gaps
- No public evidence found on whether any Indian startup already combines DM automation with art checkout. A targeted search could not be run because the search budget was exhausted.

## (D) Artist value: meaningful outcomes vs vanity

### Takeaway
The meaningful outcome is money plus a verified sales record (credibility). Shop views, wishlists and "likes" on listings are vanity unless they convert. The risk is that most under-discovered artists list items, sell nothing, and churn disappointed. Existing data shows sales on art platforms are very concentrated.

### Cited Findings
- [Verified] Singulart sells about 1,700 artworks a month across about 12,000 artists (2025). — [Wikipedia: Singulart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singulart)
- [Verified] On Redbubble, one small artist made 8 sales in about 3.5 years and never reached the payout threshold. — [inspiremari.nl, 2025-07-07](https://inspiremari.nl/redbubble-is-a-horrible-platform-for-small-artists/)
- [Verified] An artist whose followers and wishlist entries "randomly disappear or reappear" on Singulart, who paid for the top plan and called its analytics "useless". — [Threads, 2024-09-10](https://www.threads.com/@saravossart/post/C_urzgZONVj)
- [Verified] Top buyers and sellers drive the majority of Etsy GMS. — [Etsy 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)

### Inferences
- [Inference, arithmetic] Singulart's figures average about 0.14 sales per artist per month (about 1.7 a year). The distribution is almost certainly skewed, so the median artist sells less, even on a curated platform with 2.6M monthly visitors.
- **Meaningful outcomes**: paid sales; repeat collectors; commissions coming in via the shop; a verified sales history and COA trail usable for grants, galleries and brands; price discovery (what sells, at what price); local pickup sales in the artist's city.
- **Vanity**: number of listed items, shop page views, saves or wishlists without purchase, follower counts on shop pages.
- **Mitigation** [Inference]: Measure "% of shop-enabled artists with ≥1 sale in 90 days" as the north-star metric, not listings. Pair the shop with commission requests (custom work), which Saatchi and Artfinder both offer.

### Gaps
- No public median (rather than mean) sales-per-artist data for Saatchi, Artfinder or Etsy art categories.

## (E) Discovery impact

### Takeaway
On its own, the Art Shop is a **monetisation feature with weak discovery impact**: it converts the attention the artist already has (mostly from Instagram). If buyer-facing shop surfaces rank by sales, the shop will reinforce popularity bias, as Etsy's ad-blended search and Saatchi's saturation show. Fair-exposure mechanics are needed if the shop is to serve discovery at all.

### Cited Findings
- [Verified] Etsy blends onsite ads into organic search and says top sellers drive the majority of GMS. — [Etsy 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)
- [Verified] "If you are not getting likes and shares on Instagram or YouTube, you will likely not get discovered on Saatchi Art." Saatchi also recommends similar works to buyers, so artists who bring their own traffic can lose collectors to other artists. — [Very Private Gallery](https://veryprivategallery.com/what-is-saatchi-art/)
- [Verified] Online channels do reach new buyers: dealers report 40% of online sales value going to new buyers (46% for the smallest dealers). — [Art Basel & UBS 2026](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf)
- [Verified] An emerging competitor markets "Equal rotation guaranteed" and a "Fair discovery" page. — [Solene Haus](https://www.solenehaus.com/blog/art-platform-commission-comparison-2026)
- [Verified] Society6's December 2025 changes removed artist About pages "to improve discoverability" for SEO, showing how POD platforms trade artist identity for product search. — [Society6 Dec 2025 update](https://help.society6.com/en-US/updates-to-society6-artists-account---december-29-2025-3897034)

### Inferences
- **New and unknown artist exposure: Low** unless buyer surfaces exist. **Recommendation algorithms**: purchase signals are strong, high-intent data and could improve "artists like this" recommendations once volume exists. **Serendipitous discovery**: possible through "more from emerging artists" modules on checkout and thank-you pages. **Search**: buyers could search by medium, size, price and city. **Local discovery: Medium-High potential** through local pickup and city filters (a real differentiator in India's metro and tier-2 scenes) [Inference]. **Style and genre discovery**: listing metadata (medium, style) adds structured data. **Curator and brand discovery**: verified sales records help curators and brands assess artists. **Collaboration discovery**: none.
- **Popularity bias risk: High** if collections are ranked by sales or revenue, or if paid boosts are sold. The Etsy pattern is ads moving into organic results.
- **Fair-discovery ideas** [Inference]: (1) No sales-ranked leaderboards. Rank buyer surfaces by relevance plus an "exposure deficit" boost for artists with few impressions. (2) Rotating "First Sale" and "Under ₹5,000 by emerging artists" collections with per-artist exposure caps. (3) Quotas for artists under a follower threshold in curated or editorial picks. (4) City-based local-pickup feeds. (5) No paid placement until fairness metrics (impression Gini, share of exposure to artists with fewer than 1k followers) are monitored. (6) Don't show competing artists on an artist's own shop page. This avoids Saatchi's "losing your collectors" problem and keeps artists willing to share their shop links.

### Gaps
- No public data on what share of marketplace sales come from the artist's own external traffic vs platform discovery (Saatchi, Artfinder and Etsy do not publish this).

## (F) Artist behaviour analysis

### Takeaway
Artists would use the shop occasionally (listing when they have stock) and return when orders arrive. They would actively share shop links externally, which is good for acquisition, but there is little reason to invite other artists. The creator loop only closes if buyers who arrive for one artist are shown other emerging artists.

### Cited Findings
- [Verified] Etsy buyers purchase about three times a year on average, and half purchase once a year. — [Etsy 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)
- [Verified] Custom or made-to-order merchandise is about 30% of Etsy GMS, so commissions and custom work are a large share of "handmade" demand. — [Etsy 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)
- [Verified] Instagram is where art buyers browse (74%) and increasingly buy (29%). — [Hiscox 2023](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2023-04/Hiscox%20online%20art%20trade%20report%202023.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Why use it**: get paid from Instagram interest without DM back-and-forth; look professional (checkout, COA, tracking); sell prints and merch without stock (POD); sell locally (pickup).
- **Frequency**: listing is occasional, around launches or new series. Order handling is per sale (rare to weekly). Daily use is unlikely except for high-volume sellers.
- **Create content for it**: yes. Product photos, mockups, and Instagram posts or reels with a "comment SHOP"-style trigger feeding DM automation (inference; Underdawg's DM automation makes this natural).
- **Return to the app**: yes when there are orders (notifications, dispatch, payout). No when there are no sales, which is the risk for most emerging artists.
- **Invite other artists**: low. Possible if group shops or "collective drops" exist.
- **Share externally**: high. Shop links in bio, Instagram, WhatsApp. Each share is a branded Underdawg touchpoint for buyers, a modest acquisition loop.
- **Network effect**: weak. There is a cross-side effect only if a buyer audience accumulates and is cross-sold.
- **Possible creator loop**: Artist posts work on Instagram → DM automation sends a shop link → buyer purchases on Underdawg (prepaid, tracked, COA) → thank-you page and email show "more emerging artists in this style or city" → buyer follows or buys another artist → that artist sees Underdawg-originated sales → more artists enable shops and share links → more buyer traffic → better recommendations.

### Gaps
- No behavioural data on Instagram comment-to-DM checkout conversion for art (Meta and automation vendors' data not retrieved).

## (H) Risks and how competitors handle them

### Takeaway
The Art Shop carries the heaviest operational and legal load of any Underdawg feature. The main exposures are payments held in escrow (tax obligations as an e-commerce operator), damage and disputes on one-of-one items, COD return-to-origin losses, IP and copycat listings, AI-generated or misrepresented work, and fraud. Competitors handle these by holding funds for 14–21 days after delivery, managing shipping themselves, curating sellers, and through fee tiers.

### Cited Findings
- **Payment holds**: Saatchi releases funds 14–19 days after delivery ([Saatchi whysell](https://www.saatchiart.com/whysell)). Artfinder pays 14–21 days after delivery on average ([Artfinder plans](https://sellers.artfinder.com/article/927-what-are-artfinders-seller-plan-subscriptions)). Society6 holds earnings in a 30-day clearance ([Society6](https://help.society6.com/en-US/articles/pricing-earnings-258221)).
- **Shipping control**: Saatchi books its own courier pickup and the collector pays shipping. Artfinder offers managed shipping through its partner Arta; managed-shipping users are not paid the shipping price. — [Saatchi](https://www.saatchiart.com/whysell); [Artfinder paid](https://sellers.artfinder.com/article/481-how-much-will-i-be-paid)
- **Damage**: 11% of buyers reported transit damage in 2020, versus 31% in 2019. — [Hiscox 2020 Part 3](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2021-04/hoatr_report_2020_part3.pdf)
- **Returns**: Singulart offers free returns ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singulart)). Of platforms offering returns in 2020, 77% made the buyer pay return shipping and 14% offered free returns ([Hiscox 2020 Part 3](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2021-04/hoatr_report_2020_part3.pdf)).
- **IP and counterfeits**: Etsy regularly receives infringement claims (see section A). — [Etsy 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)
- **Quality and AI influx**: Society6 moved to a curated, approval-based model with a "smaller pool of artists". — [CatCoq](https://catcoq.com/blog/society6-shutting-down-artist-accounts); [Society6](https://help.society6.com/en-US/articles/pricing-earnings-258221)
- **COD/RTO** (India): COD return-to-origin runs 24–35% versus about 3–8% for prepaid orders (section J).
- **Tax compliance** (India): an e-commerce operator that collects payment must collect 0.5% TCS, file GSTR-8, and register in every state where its suppliers are located. Unregistered sellers can only sell within their own state (section J). — [ONDC GST guidance note](https://ondc-static-website-media.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/res/daea2fs3n/image/upload/ondc-website/files/ONDC_Guidance_On_Tax/ondc_note_on_unregistered_and_composition_sellers_1.pdf)
- **Platform trust failures**: Singulart artists complain of money "parked in their account while they do 'investigations' for several months". — [SmartCustomer](https://www.smartcustomer.com/reviews/singulart.com)

### Inferences
- **Fraud** [Inference]: buyer-side chargebacks or card fraud on high-value originals; seller-side non-delivery or bait-and-switch; collusive fake sales to manufacture "verified sales" credibility. Controls: prepaid only for originals, tracking-required release, and holding back "verified sale" badges until delivery plus a no-dispute window.
- **Damage and disputes**: packaging standards (corner protectors, rigid backing), a mandatory pre-dispatch "packed item" photo, an unboxing-video requirement for damage claims, and courier insurance or declared value for high-value works.
- **Copyright and plagiarism**: reverse-image matching against Underdawg's corpus and the web on listing; a takedown flow; banning POD listings of fan art or trademarks.
- **AI-generated art**: require a medium declaration, process photos for originals, and labelling of AI-assisted works. COAs only for original physical works.
- **Ops complexity**: escrow, disputes, GSTR-8 and multi-state GST registration are heavy for an early-stage startup. This is the strongest argument for phasing (section K).
- **Clutter and fatigue**: a shop with no sales becomes dead weight. Hide empty shops from discovery surfaces.

### Gaps
- Artist-side Reddit evidence on DM payment scams, shipping damage and POD copycats could not be verified (see section A gaps).
- No public data on Saatchi or Artfinder dispute or damage rates.

## (I) Scale dependency (1k / 10k / 100k artists / large scale)

### Takeaway
The seller tools (checkout, shipping booking, POD, mockups, COA) work as single-player tools from day one. The buyer-side marketplace (browse, recommendations, collections) and its discovery value need large artist supply and, more importantly, buyer traffic. That arrives only at roughly 100k artists or with a dedicated buyer-acquisition effort.

### Cited Findings
- [Verified] Even at Singulart's scale (12,000 artists, 2.6M visitors a month), monthly sales are about 1,700 artworks. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singulart)
- [Verified] Artfinder reaches 400k monthly visits with about 4,000 artists; Saatchi about 2M monthly visits. — [Artfinder](https://sellers.artfinder.com/article/927-what-are-artfinders-seller-plan-subscriptions); [Saatchi](https://www.saatchiart.com/whysell)

### Inferences
- **1,000 artists**: the Shop-lite tool works (DM automation to payment link, POD integration, mockups, COA). No buyer marketplace. Manual ops for disputes are fine. Measure the sell-through rate.
- **10,000 artists**: cross-artist recommendations on checkout and thank-you pages become meaningful within popular mediums and cities. Local-pickup feeds are viable in the top 5–8 metros. Disputes and support need a dedicated person. GST and TCS processes need to be automated if escrow is used.
- **100,000 artists**: a buyer-facing "shop emerging art" destination becomes plausible (comparable to Saatchi's claimed reach only if buyer acquisition succeeds). Fairness controls and fraud detection become mandatory; consider insurance partnerships.
- **Large scale**: international shipping (export documentation, customs), financing or EMI for higher-priced works (Hiscox: 37% want interest-free repayment plans), and an advisory service (79% of new buyers want to talk to an advisor) start to make sense.

### Gaps
- No public benchmarks on buyer-traffic thresholds at which art marketplaces become self-sustaining.

## (J) India specifics: shipping, COD/RTO, packaging, international, GST/TCS/TDS, Etsy status, payments

### Takeaway
India makes self-shipping cheap (aggregators average about ₹36–45 per shipment for typical parcels), but COD is the default for e-commerce (about 60–65% of orders) and COD orders return-to-origin about 24–35% of the time. For one-of-one originals, COD should be disallowed. The bigger structural constraint is GST. If Underdawg collects money (escrow), it is an e-commerce operator that must collect TCS and file returns, and **unregistered artists may only sell to buyers in their own state**. Most emerging artists are unregistered, so a nationwide escrow marketplace would push them into GST registration. This one rule should shape the product design.

**TCS, TDS and GST rates**
- [Verified] GST TCS by ECOs (Section 52) was reduced from 1% to **0.5%** (0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST, or 0.5% IGST) from **10 July 2024** by CGST Notification 15/2024 and IGST Notification 01/2024. — [ClearTax (2026-10-01)](https://cleartax.in/s/tds-and-tcs-under-gst); [TaxGuru (2026-09-23)](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-tcs-commerce-operators-section-52-gstr-8-0-5-percent-rate.html)

- [Verified, legal opinion] According to a lawyer quoted in ArtSceneIndia, the 5% rate is granted on a **"without ITC"** basis, so artists and galleries can no longer offset input GST (materials, framing, logistics). — [ArtSceneIndia, Sept 2025](https://www.artsceneindia.com/2025/09/GST-on-FineArt-Benefits-and-Implications.html)

### Inferences
- [Inference, needs tax counsel] **Design fork created by GST:**
  - (a) **Escrow model.** Underdawg collects payment, so it is an ECO collecting consideration. It takes on TCS (0.5%), GSTR-8, state registrations and 194-O TDS (0.1%). Unregistered artists can sell only within their own state, and inter-state sales require artist GST registration.
  - (b) **Direct-payment model.** The buyer pays the artist directly (the artist's UPI or payment link) and Underdawg charges a SaaS or listing fee. Whether this avoids ECO TCS status depends on who "collects consideration", so get a CA's opinion.
  - (c) **POD model.** The POD partner (Qikink/Printrove) is the registered seller of goods and ships nationwide, and the artist earns a margin. This is the lowest-friction nationwide option for prints and merch.
- [Inference] COD for originals is a bad fit. A 24–35% COD RTO means the one-of-one work travels twice, raising damage risk, while the artist pays return freight (₹172–240) and loses the selling window. Recommendation: prepaid only (UPI, cards) for originals. COD only through a POD partner that absorbs RTO (Qikink: "zero RTO charges").
- [Inference] Shiprocket's ₹36–45 average cost reflects typical small parcels. A framed painting is billed on volumetric weight and will cost much more (not quantified; gap). Shipping must be priced by size and zone at listing, as the proposal already plans.
- [Inference] Etsy treats India as an export channel. A domestic-first Indian art checkout is complementary rather than head-to-head with Etsy.
- [Inference] The 5% GST cut helps registered artists' buyers on price, but the without-ITC rule may push small sellers toward informal (DM + UPI) sales. That increases the value of easy compliance tooling but also the temptation to stay off-platform.

### Gaps
- Delhivery and India Post (Speed Post) current small-parcel tariffs were not retrieved. No reliable public data captured on rates for a framed painting (volumetric).
- Export rules for contemporary art from India (Antiquities and Art Treasures Act / non-antiquity certificate practice, courier-export value limits, export invoicing) were **not verified**. Official pages could not be searched.
- Whether paintings qualify for the handicraft-goods inter-state registration exemption (a possible route for unregistered artists to sell inter-state) was **not verified**. Get a CA's opinion before relying on it.
- Packaging and damage rates for paintings in Indian courier networks: no reliable public data found.
- Instagram Shops checkout availability in India and WhatsApp in-chat payments: not verified (pages inaccessible).

## (K) Evidence-based preliminary verdict and recommended design

### Takeaway
**Verdict: MODIFY + BUILD AFTER PMF, with a small early EXPERIMENT.**
- Do **not** build the full escrow marketplace (self-ship escrow plus disputes plus COA plus POD plus AI mockups plus national inter-state sales) before Underdawg proves its discovery value. It is operationally and legally heavy (GST ECO rules, COD/RTO, damage disputes), it weakly serves the core discovery mission, and its parts exist cheaply elsewhere (Instagram DM + UPI is free; Blinkstore and Qikink give artists free POD stores; Linktree and Beacons offer link-in-bio selling).
- Do run an early **"Shop-lite" experiment** inside the link-in-bio and DM automation, because the demand signal is strong. Artist-direct and Instagram buying is growing (20% of HNW spend; 29% of online buyers have bought on Instagram; 42% of newest collectors prefer buying direct from artists), and the trust levers (COA, tracking, clear shipping, returns) are known.

### Cited Findings
- Demand and channel shift: [Art Basel & UBS 2026](https://theartmarket.artbasel.com/download/The-Art-Basel-and-UBS-Art-Market-Report-2026-by-Arts-Economics.pdf); [Hiscox 2023](https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2023-04/Hiscox%20online%20art%20trade%20report%202023.pdf)
- Fee benchmarks for bring-your-own-audience selling: Bandcamp 10% on physical goods ([Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com/pricing)); Linktree and Beacons 9% on free plans ([Linktree](https://linktr.ee/s/pricing/), [Beacons](https://beacons.ai/i/pricing)); Etsy 6.5% plus 3–5% processing plus optional ads ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees/), [Etsy India](https://www.etsy.com/in-en/sell)); Instamojo 2–5% + ₹3 ([Instamojo](https://www.instamojo.com/pricing/)); Fourthwall 0% on POD and own products ([Fourthwall](https://fourthwall.com/pricing)).
- Full-service galleries justify 40–50% only by supplying buyers, logistics and advisory: [Saatchi](https://www.saatchiart.com/whysell); [Artfinder](https://sellers.artfinder.com/article/927-what-are-artfinders-seller-plan-subscriptions); [Singulart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singulart).
- Market-standard escrow release is 14–21 days after delivery: [Saatchi](https://www.saatchiart.com/whysell); [Artfinder](https://sellers.artfinder.com/article/927-what-are-artfinders-seller-plan-subscriptions).
- India GST constraints, COD/RTO and Shiprocket costs: section J sources.

### Inferences: reasons for the verdict
1. **Weak link to the core objective.** The shop monetises attention rather than creating it (section E). Under-discovered artists with small audiences will mostly see empty shops (section D).
2. **High operational and legal cost.** An escrow marketplace makes Underdawg an ECO (TCS, GSTR-8, multi-state registration, 194-O TDS) and restricts unregistered artists to intra-state sales (section J). Disputes, damage and RTO need a support function.
3. **Commodity parts, low moat.** Every component exists. The defensible asset is the **verified-sales credibility data**, which needs only a modest transaction layer.
4. **Real but narrow pain.** The pain is real for artists who already get "price?" DMs, which is exactly what DM automation targets, so the experiment is cheap to run there.

### Recommended way the feature should work (evidence-based, phased)

**Phase 0: early experiment ("Shop-lite", within link-in-bio + DM automation)** [Inference]
- Listing: photos (min. 3 real photos; AI mockups optional and labelled "visualisation", rendered at true scale), type, medium, size, price, quantity ("1 of 1" for originals), dispatch time, shipping price by zone, local pickup.
- Checkout: **prepaid only** (UPI or cards). Preferred legal design: the buyer pays the artist directly through a payment link or gateway account in the artist's name, and Underdawg charges a small fee or none during the experiment. This avoids becoming the collecting ECO until counsel confirms the model. Alternative: Underdawg-collected payments only for GST-registered artists, plus intra-state sales for unregistered artists with an enrolment number (section J).
- Fulfilment: the artist books a Shiprocket (or similar) pickup from inside the flow, or enters their own courier's tracking number. Tracking is mandatory before an order counts as a "verified sale".
- POD: Qikink or Printrove integration for prints, tees and mugs. The POD partner is the seller and shipper, COD is allowed here only (Qikink absorbs RTO), and the artist keeps most of the margin. Underdawg takes no more than the Indian free-storefront norm (Blinkstore is free), ideally funded by a partner rev-share rather than a visible "20% of margin".
- Trust: an auto-generated digital COA for originals (unique ID, QR to the listing and sale record); clear shipping and packing info on the listing (72% of hesitant buyers want this); a stated returns policy (damaged or not-as-described only, for originals).
- Discovery hooks: "First sale" and "Under ₹5,000 by emerging artists" rotations with exposure caps; city and local-pickup feeds; no sales-ranked leaderboards; no paid boosts.
- Success metrics: share of shop-enabled artists with at least 1 sale in 90 days; DM-to-checkout conversion; on-time dispatch; dispute and damage rate; share of buyers who view or buy a second artist.

**Phase 1: after PMF (managed escrow for originals)** [Inference]
- Escrow: funds held until courier-confirmed delivery, then released after a short no-dispute window (e.g., 5–7 days, faster than Saatchi's 14–19 or Artfinder's 14–21 days as a pro-artist differentiator). Local pickup: OTP-confirmed handover with immediate release.
- Disputes: unboxing video required for damage claims; a pre-dispatch packed-item photo; courier insurance or declared value for works above a threshold; damage and loss handled with the courier.
- Tax: automated TCS and GSTR-8 handling; enrolment-number capture for unregistered intra-state sellers; GSTIN verification for inter-state sellers.
- Fee: about **8–10% all-in** (including payment processing) on originals sold through the artist's own links, consistent with Etsy, Bandcamp and Linktree benchmarks. Consider 0% on sales the artist drives themselves plus a subscription, if retention data supports it. A higher rate (15–20%) is justified only on sales Underdawg originates through its own discovery surfaces, i.e., charge more only when the platform actually brings the buyer.
- International: defer. Later, offer prints via POD partners with international shipping (Printrove: 220+ countries) before attempting originals export.

**What NOT to do** [Inference]: don't launch COD for originals; don't rank artists by sales; don't sell paid boosts early; don't show competing artists on an artist's own shop page; don't let AI mockups replace real photos.

### Gaps
- The legal treatment of the "direct-payment link" model under GST Section 52 and Section 194-O needs a CA or tax counsel opinion.
- No Indian data on DM-to-purchase conversion for art. Validate in the Phase 0 experiment.

# Indian markets and Indian platforms for an artists' discovery-and-opportunity platform (Underdawg)

Research date: 2 Oct 2026. Every source below was opened on 2 Oct 2026 unless marked "not opened". Amounts are in rupees; FICCI-EY figures are published in INR billion and converted here (INR 1 billion = ₹100 crore). Labels used: [Primary] = original report, regulator or filing; [Press] = news report of a primary source; [Company claim] = number published by the company about itself; [Inc42 Datalabs] = third-party database of company filings, indicative only; [Blog] = commercial blog, low reliability.

## 1. Live gigs and events in India: market size, number of events, city tiers, emerging artists, fees

### Takeaway
India's organised live-events segment was ₹14,500 crore in 2025 (up 44%, the fastest-growing media segment), but the money that is actually tracked sits in stadium concerts, weddings and government events in the top 10 metros; nobody publishes a figure for the café, pub, college and small-wedding circuit where unknown artists work. The only rupee fee data for emerging artists comes from booking-platform blogs: roughly ₹3,000–15,000 for a café gig, ₹5,000–30,000 for a college fest, ₹20,000–75,000 for an emerging singer, DJ, dancer or comedian at a private or corporate event.

### Cited Findings

**Market size by year (FICCI-EY)**
- Organised live events segment, INR billion: 2022: 73; 2023: 88; 2024: 101; 2025: 145; 2026E: 140; 2028E: 196; CAGR 2025–2028 10%. That is ₹7,300 crore → ₹8,800 crore → ₹10,100 crore → ₹14,500 crore → ₹14,000 crore (E) → ₹19,600 crore (E). [Primary] — [FICCI-EY, "Stories, scale and impact", March 2026, segment table p.~10](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/2026/03/ey-stories-scale-and-impact-un-locking-indias-media-and-entertainment-economy.pdf) (24 Mar 2026)
- 2019 (pre-COVID) organised live events were INR 83 billion (₹8,300 crore); the March 2025 report forecast only INR 119 billion for 2025, so 2025 beat forecast by about ₹2,600 crore. [Primary] — [FICCI-EY, "Shape the future", March 2025](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/documents/ey-shape-the-future-indian-media-and-entertainment-is-scripting-a-new-story.pdf) (Mar 2025)
- The 44% growth in 2025 was "supported by the Kumbh Mela that added INR25 billion of direct event production spend, and a significant growth in concerts and ticketed events". Net of Kumbh, EY expects 18% growth to 2028 and "a slight dip in 2026 due to a rationalisation in concerts". [Primary/Press] — [FICCI-EY 2026 PDF](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/2026/03/ey-stories-scale-and-impact-un-locking-indias-media-and-entertainment-economy.pdf); [MediaNews4U summary](https://www.medianews4u.com/live-events-growth-shifts-beyond-metros-next-10-cities-lead-expansion-wave-ficci-ey-report/) (Mar 2026)
- The organised figure is a small slice of all events. FICCI-EY's own sizing chart for 2025 shows organised events at INR 145 billion against an "unaddressable events market (weddings, religious, personal, etc.)" of INR 1,118 billion (₹1,11,800 crore). Two further bars of INR 109 billion and INR 50 billion cover "events done by media companies" and "in-house corporate events, trainings, offsites"; the PDF text layer does not show which value belongs to which label. The organised number explicitly excludes "revenues of the multitude of 'unorganized' event companies" and "personal events paid for in cash". [Primary] — [FICCI-EY 2026 PDF, live events chapter](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/2026/03/ey-stories-scale-and-impact-un-locking-indias-media-and-entertainment-economy.pdf) (Mar 2026)
- BookMyShow and EY-Parthenon put the live events segment at "surpassing the INR12,000 crore mark in 2024" with growth of "around 19% over the next three years" [Primary, commissioned by BookMyShow] — [EY-Parthenon/BookMyShow, "India's rising concert economy"](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/ey-india-s-rising-concert-economy.pdf) (2025); a later BookMyShow–EY-Parthenon report puts it at ₹13,000 crore [Press] — [IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/news/india-s-us-1-41-billion-live-events-market-fuelling-experiential-marketing-push-bookmyshow-ey-report) (2025–26). Conflict: FICCI-EY's 2024 figure is ₹10,100 crore; the two EY outputs use different scopes.

**Independent and emerging artists' share**
- No source gives independent or emerging artists' share of live-event revenue or of event count. The closest Indian data points: non-film ("independent") music was 43% of streams in 2025 (IPRS estimate in FICCI-EY 2026); in a 2022 IPRS-EY survey of 500 music creators, "live performances (incl. DJing)" was ranked the top revenue source by 139 respondents, the highest rank-1 count of any source (upfront fees from labels and film producers: 129). [Primary] — [FICCI-EY 2026 PDF](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/2026/03/ey-stories-scale-and-impact-un-locking-indias-media-and-entertainment-economy.pdf); [EY, "The music creator economy", 2023](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/documents/ey-the-music-creator-economy.pdf) (survey 2022, published Dec 2023)

### Inferences
- The tracked market (₹14,500 crore) and the untracked one (₹1,11,800 crore of weddings, religious and personal events) differ by about 7.7 times. Unknown artists earn in the untracked part, which is where a discovery product has no incumbent data owner.
- BookMyShow lists about 34,000 events a year while EY counts about 14,470 concerts in 2024 and only 130 large concert days; most events are therefore small. The small-venue long tail is large in count and invisible in rupees.
- Fee floors of ₹3,000–15,000 (café) and ₹5,000–30,000 (college) mean a 15% platform fee yields ₹450–4,500 per booking; a flat subscription near ₹299 a month would equal the commission on one small gig.
- EY names a "trust deficit", "unorganized competition" and "talent scarcity" as the three event-industry problems. All three are directory-and-reputation problems.
- The 2026 concert correction (cancelled and unsold shows in Q4 2025) raises the risk of pitching Underdawg on the "concert boom"; the wedding, college and corporate circuit is steadier, with 92% of event firms expecting growth in weddings.

### Gaps
- No reliable Indian data found on: the number of college fests in India or their total entertainment budgets; the number of cafés and pubs hosting live music; independent or emerging artists' share of live revenue or bookings; how organisers find artists (no survey located); corporate-event entertainment spend; the entertainment share of wedding budgets.
- District by Zomato's own year-end numbers (events, tickets, cities) were not found in an open source; only the Music Ally summary of "Touching Grass" was available. Skillbox has not published a market report that I could open.
- A widely circulated claim that "the top 20 college festivals have a combined budget exceeding ₹200 crore and ₹85 crore of sponsorship" appears only on an SEO blog (blog.brainfox.in, not opened) and should not be used.
- The 109 vs 50 split between "events by media companies" and "in-house corporate events" in FICCI-EY's 2025 sizing chart needs a visual check of the PDF.
- FICCI-EY's "28% GST on event tickets" line may not match the September 2025 GST rate changes; check against CBIC notifications before quoting.

## 2. Artist-booking and talent platforms in India

### Takeaway
Indian artist-booking platforms are small and old: StarClinch (founded 2015) reports about ₹2.4 crore of FY25 revenue and charges artists 15% plus a ₹7.5 lakh penalty for going direct; Talentrack and Skillbox are bigger (₹36.4 crore and ₹29.9 crore) but earn from brand content and ticketing, not from finding work for unknown artists. None publishes how many of its registered artists actually got booked.

### Cited Findings
- **StarClinch (VINSM Globe Pvt Ltd, New Delhi, founded 2015).** Terms: "We will charge 15% of the remuneration received by the Artist towards the assignments which the Artists get through us as our service fee… StarClinch does not guarantee you any work." Direct deals between registered clients and artists without permission make "each of the Artist and Client… liable to pay StarClinch a penalty of INR 750,000". [Primary, company terms] — [StarClinch terms of use](https://starclinch.com/terms-of-use) (accessed 2 Oct 2026)
- StarClinch's "our story" page shows "10K+" and "Since 2015"; categories are celebrity, comedian, dancer/troupe, DJ, anchor, instrumentalist, live band, magician, make-up artist, model, photo/videographer, singer, speaker, variety artist and influencer. An earlier research note in this project recorded "17,000+ artists across 14 categories and 450+ cities" from the same page on 1 Oct 2026; I could not reproduce that figure. [Company claim] — [StarClinch](https://starclinch.com/our-story) (accessed 2 Oct 2026)
- StarClinch revenue ₹2.4 crore in FY25, down 10.2% from ₹2.7 crore in FY24; total funding $233,120 in one round (23 Dec 2021); 38 employees. [Inc42 Datalabs] — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/starclinch/) (accessed 2 Oct 2026)
- **Talentrack (New Delhi, founded 2015).** "Access a vast network of 50K+ artists, experts, and influencers"; "20K+ curated Influencers"; "3,000+ voiceover artists"; brand budget bands "Up to ₹2L", "₹2L–₹5L", "₹5L–₹10L", "₹10L+". [Company claim] — [Talentrack](https://www.talentrack.in) (accessed 2 Oct 2026). Revenue ₹36.4 crore in FY25, up 17.6% from ₹30.9 crore. [Inc42 Datalabs] — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/talentrack/)
- **Skillbox (Gurugram, founded 2017 per Inc42).** Began as "a digital space for musicians to share their art", "evolved to include features for event management and ticketing"; revenue ₹29.9 crore in FY25, up 19.0% from ₹25.1 crore; 37 employees. [Inc42 Datalabs] — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/skillbox/) (accessed 2 Oct 2026)
- **IndieFolio (Mumbai, founded 2015).** Positions as "Access India's top UX/UI talent on demand", a managed brand-to-freelancer service with "50+ services" and a talent application flow; it is no longer an open portfolio network. [Company claim] — [IndieFolio](https://home.indiefolio.com) (accessed 2 Oct 2026)
- **Gigstart (Mumbai, artist booking).** Raised $210,000 in one round (4 Dec 2014); last reported revenue ₹11.7 lakh in FY16, down 50.7% from ₹23.7 lakh in FY15. [Inc42 Datalabs] — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/gigstart/) (accessed 2 Oct 2026)
- **BookMyShow** is the dominant ticketing listing (34,086 events in 2025) and acts as promoter for large tours (Coldplay, Ed Sheeran, Post Malone, Lollapalooza India); it has signed MoUs with the tourism departments of Assam, Telangana, Gujarat and Delhi. [Press] — [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2025/12/11/over-34k-events-mous-and-record-breaking-gigs-bookmyshows-year-in-live-entertainment/) (11 Dec 2025)
- **District by Zomato** hands out collectible "District Cards" at gigs (Akon and Enrique Iglesias tours, Rolling Loud India); its "Touching Grass" report notes both of Enrique Iglesias's Mumbai shows in October 2025 "were attended by over 25,000 people despite being staged on a Wednesday and Thursday night". [Press] — [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2026/02/04/district-by-zomatos-touching-grass-report-tracks-trends-in-indias-concert-going-culture/) (4 Feb 2026)
- Event-industry economics: over half of surveyed event companies earn margins of 10–30%, and the top challenges are "unorganized competition, talent scarcity and a trust deficit". [Primary] — [FICCI-EY 2026 PDF](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/2026/03/ey-stories-scale-and-impact-un-locking-indias-media-and-entertainment-economy.pdf) (Mar 2026)

### Inferences
- At a 15% fee, StarClinch's ₹2.4 crore revenue implies about ₹16 crore of artist bookings a year if all revenue were commission (my arithmetic; the revenue mix is not disclosed). That is 0.1% of the ₹14,500 crore organised market, so the booking-marketplace model has not scaled in ten years.
- What these platforms do not do for unknown artists, on the evidence of their own pages: no guarantee of work (StarClinch terms), no public proof of how many artists get booked, no audience-building or Instagram tooling, and a penalty rather than an incentive for repeat direct business.
- The bigger Indian "talent" businesses (Talentrack, Skillbox) grew by serving brands and ticket buyers. An artists-only product competes for the artist's attention, not for their revenue line.

### Gaps
- Hire4Event: the site returned HTTP 403 and Inc42 has no data; no reliable figures found.
- BookMyShow and District organiser-tool fees (convenience fee or commission to organisers) were not found on an open page; no reliable Indian data found.
- No reliable Indian data found on complaints volumes (consumer-forum or app-store review counts) for StarClinch or Talentrack. Play Store review pages were not opened.
- StarClinch's "escrow-style" payment wording was not on the pages I opened; treat as unverified in this note.

## 3. Brand–creator platforms in India

### Takeaway
India has 4.0–4.4 million active creators and an influencer-marketing market of ₹3,000–3,500 crore (2025), but only 8–10% of creators monetise effectively and most nano and micro creators earn under ₹18,000 a month. The Indian brand–creator platforms claim very large rosters (Kofluence 750,000+, Hobo.Video 225,187+, WYLD 100,000+) while earning ₹50–56 crore a year at the top, so rosters far exceed paid work.

### Cited Findings
- Market size: India's influencer marketing sector "is valued at ₹3,000-3,500 Cr in 2025, sustaining a 22% CAGR… projected to reach ₹4,500-5,000 Cr by 2027". Creator base "4.0M-4.4M+ active professionals", Instagram "primary infrastructure for 3.3-3.7 million creators"; 61.1% of surveyed creators are nano (1K–10K followers); 15.2% are "registered as a business entity or GST individual"; 93.1% of brands prioritise Instagram; e-commerce is 23% and FMCG 19% of sector spend. Based on "data from over 2 million creators, 1,000+ surveys". [Company report/Press] — [Adgully on Kofluence "Decoding Influence 2026"](https://www.adgully.com/post/15568/kofluence-launches-2026-influencer-marketing-report) (2026)
- Campaign cost by city tier (Kofluence 2026): metros, engagement 3–4%, average cost per campaign ₹3.8–4.5 lakh; Tier 2, engagement 3.5–4.5%, ₹1.3–1.6 lakh; Tier 3 and 4, engagement 4.5–5.5% (cost truncated in the article). 62% of creators report more regional-language briefs. [Company report/Press] — [Adgully](https://www.adgully.com/post/15568/kofluence-launches-2026-influencer-marketing-report) (2026)
- How many earn: "an estimated 450K to 600K creators are currently monetising their content in some form"; revenue from platforms (YouTube, Meta) is "around 15%" of income, sponsored collaborations the primary source for nearly half. FY 2024–25 short-form earnings by tier: nano (1K–10K) ₹300–5,000; micro (10K–100K) ₹3,000–1,00,000 on Instagram, up to ₹2,00,000 on YouTube; macro (100K–500K) ₹75,000–5,00,000 "annually"; over 500K often more than ₹3,00,000 (the source does not state the period for the other tiers). Branded content starts "from Rs 500 for nano-influencers". Average spend per activation in media and entertainment ₹12–18 lakh. [Company claim, interview] — [IBTimes India, interview with Kofluence CEO](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/how-much-do-creators-really-earn-kofluence-ceo-decodes-indias-influencer-economy-interview-885077) (2025)

## 4. Art sales in India

### Takeaway
Indian art auction turnover hit a decade high of ₹2,543 crore in 2025 (from ₹792 crore in 2015), but about 92% of value is Post-War and Modern art (₹2,348 crore of ₹2,543 crore; my arithmetic); contemporary art was ₹163 crore. ### Inferences
- Contemporary art is 6.4% of 2025 auction value (₹163 crore of ₹2,543 crore, my arithmetic). The auction boom is not income for living, unknown artists.
- From the price bands, 371 lots sold above ₹1 crore (4,057 minus 3,686; my arithmetic), and those lots carry most of the value. The mass of activity, 2,399 lots, is under ₹10 lakh.
- An artist selling a ₹20,000 work on Artflute keeps ₹12,000 before paying domestic shipping and packaging, then waits up to 15 working days after delivery. That is the benchmark an artist-first shop has to beat.
- The one-state rule for unregistered sellers means an unregistered artist cannot legally ship a painting to another state through a marketplace. Most hobby and emerging artists are below the ₹40 lakh threshold, so this is the binding constraint on an art shop, not the 5% rate.
- A 26% return-to-origin rate on cash-on-delivery orders against under 2% for prepaid argues for prepaid-only art sales.

## 5. Music in India

### Takeaway
Music labels' revenue was ₹5,900 crore in 2025 (+10%), with only 14.4 million paid subscribers out of about 178 million streamers; non-film music is now 43% of streams, and IPRS collected a record ₹804.5 crore in FY2025–26 and paid ₹628.3 crore to 13,700+ members. For an unknown musician the live gig remains the top income source, while streaming rates and thresholds worsened in 2025.

## 6. Creative freelance hiring in India

### Takeaway
Official data counts India's gig workforce (7.7 million in 2020–21, projected 23.5 million by 2029–30) but nothing isolates creative freelancers. Payment-delay evidence in India is weak: the most-quoted figure (58% of freelancers have gone unpaid) is an unattributed line on a payments company's blog.

### Cited Findings
- NITI Aayog: "in 2020–21, 77 lakh (7.7 million) workers were engaged in the gig economy", 2.6% of the non-agricultural workforce; expected "2.35 crore (23.5 million) workers by 2029–30" (6.7% of the non-agricultural workforce); about 47% medium-skilled, 22% high-skilled, 31% low-skilled. [Primary; 2022] — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1837277&reg=48&lang=2) (27 Jun 2022)

[Tertiary; older] — [Wikipedia, Freelancing in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freelancing_in_India)

### Inferences
- With 68% of freelancers finding work through social media, Instagram is already the hiring surface; the gap is trust and payment, not discovery alone.

## 10. Indian rails and rules: UPI, payment aggregators, GST/TCS/TDS, DPDP, IT Rules, minors

### Takeaway
UPI handled 24,162 crore transactions worth ₹314 lakh crore in FY2025–26, but from 15 October 2026 merchant payments above ₹2,000 carry a 0.4% fee (capped at ₹300), ending zero-cost UPI for most artist bookings. A platform cannot hold other people's money itself: only an RBI-authorised payment aggregator (₹15 crore net worth, rising to ₹25 crore) may pool funds in escrow, and such an aggregator "shall not carry out marketplace business".

### Cited Findings

**UPI**
- UPI volume and value by financial year: FY2021–22: 4,595.61 crore transactions, ₹84.16 lakh crore; FY2022–23: 8,371.44 crore, ₹139.15 lakh crore; FY2023–24: 13,112.95 crore, ₹199.95 lakh crore; FY2024–25: 18,586.60 crore, ₹260.56 lakh crore; FY2025–26: 24,161.69 crore, ₹314.23 lakh crore. 55.49 crore users onboarded as of June 2026. [Press of Lok Sabha written reply] — [Punjab Kesari/IANS](https://english.punjabkesari.com/business/nearly-555-crore-users-onboarded-on-upi-by-june-fy26-transactions-cross-24161-crore-centre) (20 Jul 2026)
- FY2025–26: 66 crore transactions a day on average, ₹0.86 lakh crore a day; 703 banks live (March 2026); person-to-merchant (P2M) payments are 63% of volume while person-to-person is 71% of value; 86% of P2M payments are below ₹500; FY2016–17 volume was 2 crore. [Primary] — [PIB, "UPI completes 10 years"](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2257087&reg=3&lang=2) (Aug 2026)
- Merchant fee from 15 October 2026: "a 0.4% Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on merchant transactions above ₹2,000", "capped at ₹300 for transactions above ₹75,000"; railways, telecom, insurance and fuel pay a flat ₹5; person-to-person transfers and merchant payments up to ₹2,000 stay free; "small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month through QR-code payments directly into their accounts will also remain exempt"; merchants may not pass the charge to customers. [Press of Ministry of Finance announcement] — [The Indian Eye](https://theindianeye.com/2026/09/18/upi-charges-to-apply-on-large-merchant-payments-from-october-15/) (18 Sep 2026); [Digital Watch Observatory](https://dig.watch/updates/india-sets-merchant-discount-rate-on-upi-payments) (Sep 2026); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-govt-role-upi-mdr-gst/) (Sep 2026)
- Trader bodies oppose the fee: AIMRA called a "No UPI Day" on 2 October 2026 with at least five other trade bodies. [Press] — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-govt-role-upi-mdr-gst/) (Sep 2026)

**RBI payment-aggregator rules (holding money)**
- RBI (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions, 2025, issued 15 September 2025, effective immediately. A non-bank needs RBI authorisation to operate as a payment aggregator; minimum net worth ₹15 crore at application and ₹25 crore by the end of the third financial year. "A non-bank PA shall maintain the funds collected on behalf of its merchants in a separate escrow account with any Scheduled Commercial Bank"; the day-end escrow balance must not be less than amounts due to merchants; no interest except on the core portion; "the escrow account shall not be operated for 'Cash-on-Delivery' transactions"; refunds route back through escrow; "a PA business shall not carry out marketplace business"; a PA must ensure a marketplace it onboards "does not accept payments for a seller not onboarded on to the marketplace's platform". [Primary text reproduced] — [TaxGuru, full text of the Directions](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-regulation-payment-aggregators-directions-2025.html) (Sep 2025)

**GST, TCS and TDS for platforms and small sellers**
- GST TCS: e-commerce operators collect 0.5% on net taxable supplies made through them (from 10 July 2024; 1% before) and must register for GST with no threshold. [Explainer] — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/tcs-under-goods-and-services-tax)
- Unregistered goods sellers may sell through e-commerce operators within one state after GST-portal enrolment (Notification 34/2023); no inter-state supply; PAN required. [Explainer] — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/facility-enrolment-supply-goods-e-commerce-operators-gst-un-registered-suppliers.html)
- Registration thresholds: ₹40 lakh turnover for goods; ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in special-category states) otherwise. [Explainer] — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-registration-limits-increased)
- Income-tax TDS by e-commerce operators: 0.1% of gross sales; exempt for resident individuals and HUFs up to ₹5 lakh a year with PAN or Aadhaar; 5% without. Now Section 393(1) of the Income Tax Act 2025. [Explainers] — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/section-194o); [Tax Garden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/tds-on-ecommerce-payments-section-194o-393-guide-india-fy-2026-27)
- GST on art: 5% from 22 September 2025 (was 12%). [Press] — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/gst-rate-cut-from-12-to-5-poised-to-brighten-india-s-art-landscape-125092400909_1.html) (24 Sep 2025)

**DPDP Act and Rules**
- DPDP Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 with "an eighteen-month period for phased compliance". Penalties: up to ₹250 crore for failing to maintain reasonable security safeguards; up to ₹200 crore each for not notifying a breach and for violating children's obligations; up to ₹50 crore for other violations. Breaches must be notified to affected individuals "without delay" in plain language. "When a child's personal data is involved, verifiable consent from a parent or guardian is required." [Primary] — [PIB explainer, "DPDP Rules, 2025 Notified"](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf) (17 Nov 2025)
- The Act defines a child as "an individual who has not completed the age of eighteen years" and says "a Data Fiduciary shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children". [Primary] — [DPDP Act 2023, MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) (Aug 2023)

**IT Rules for user-content platforms**
- IT Rules 2021: every intermediary must appoint a Grievance Officer who acknowledges complaints within 24 hours and resolves them within 15 days; content showing nudity, sexual acts or impersonation (including morphed images) must be removed within 24 hours of a complaint; significant social media intermediaries must appoint an India-resident Chief Compliance Officer and meet further duties. [Primary] — [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=1700749) (25 Feb 2021)
- IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026 (notified February 2026): unlawful information must be removed "within 3 hours, which was earlier 36 hours" of actual knowledge; AI-generated ("synthetically generated") visual content must carry "a clear and noticeable label" with metadata users cannot remove; significant social media intermediaries must make users declare AI-generated content before publishing and verify the declaration. [Legal press] — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/02/12/it-rules-2026-ai-and-intermediary-compliance/) (12 Feb 2026); [Argus Partners](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/update-overview-of-the-it-intermediary-amendment-rules-2026/) (Feb 2026)

### Inferences
- Average UPI ticket size has fallen every year: about ₹1,831 (FY22), ₹1,662 (FY23), ₹1,525 (FY24), ₹1,402 (FY25), ₹1,301 (FY26) (value divided by volume; my arithmetic). Typical artist payments (₹3,000–75,000 per gig, ₹1 lakh+ artworks) sit above the ₹2,000 line and will now cost the receiving merchant 0.4%, about ₹12 on ₹3,000 and ₹300 on ₹75,000 or more.
- An artist paid person-to-person, or one who qualifies as a small merchant (up to ₹1 lakh a month by QR into their own account), pays nothing. A platform that collects centrally as a merchant pays the fee. This changes the cost comparison between "pay the artist directly by UPI" and "pay through the platform".
- "Escrow" for bookings must be delivered through a licensed payment aggregator's split-settlement or a bank escrow; Underdawg cannot pool client money in its own account, and a PA cannot itself be the marketplace. Use the word "escrow" only for the regulated arrangement.
- If Underdawg collects payments for artists it is an e-commerce operator: GST registration from day one, 0.5% TCS returns, 0.1% TDS above ₹5 lakh per artist per year. Unregistered artists selling goods through it are limited to buyers in their own state.
- Any user under 18 needs verifiable parental consent, and the platform may not track them or target ads at them; penalties reach ₹200 crore. Full Data Fiduciary duties apply about 18 months after 14 November 2025, around May 2027 (my calculation). An 18+ sign-up rule is the low-cost route for launch.
- A user-content platform needs a named Grievance Officer, 24-hour acknowledgement, 15-day resolution, 24-hour removal for intimate or impersonation content, a 3-hour removal path for unlawful content, and AI-content labelling from launch, whatever its size.

### Gaps
- The user threshold for "significant social media intermediary" (widely reported as 50 lakh registered users in India) was not found in the pages I opened; confirm against the MeitY gazette notification.
- NPCI's statistics page returned HTTP 403; figures come from PIB and a press report of a parliamentary reply. The Ministry of Finance notification on UPI MDR was not opened directly, only three press reports of it.
- RBI's settlement-timeline wording (T+1) and the detailed DPDP Rules on verifiable consent methods and breach-reporting hours were not verified in the texts opened.
- Whether services sold through a platform by unregistered artists (sessions, commissions) attract compulsory GST registration below ₹20 lakh needs a tax adviser; the explainers opened cover goods.
- No Indian rule specific to minors performing or selling on platforms (beyond DPDP) was researched in this pass.
